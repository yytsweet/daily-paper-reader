## ABSTRACT 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have achieved strong performance on robot manipulation benchmarks. Translating this progress into reliable deployment calls for systematic evaluation of how policies respond to variations throughout the robot interaction loop. We introduce LIBERO-MOR , a multimodal robustness benchmark across the vision-language-action pipeline. Built on standard LIBERO, it covers five deployment-facing domains: vision, language, proprioception, action execution, and environment. The benchmark comprises 22 deployment-inspired perturbations, each evaluated at three severity levels across 40 tasks under a paired clean–perturbed protocol, enabling controlled and reproducible comparisons. We evaluate seven representative VLA models spanning diverse action-generation mechanisms. Our experiments reveal substantial robustness gaps behind high clean-task success, together with shared vulnerabilities and distinct model-specific failure patterns across domains. Severity analysis further distinguishes progressive degradation, collapse at stronger interventions, and failures triggered at the mildest tested level. By connecting aggregate performance to pipeline domains, individual perturbations, and intervention strength, LIBERO-MOR provides a systematic framework for diagnosing VLA robustness and guiding the development of more reliable robot policies. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models combine visual and linguistic representations with robot control, using action-generation mechanisms ranging from autoregressive decoding to regression, diffusion, and flow matching (Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Black et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2025). Benchmarks such as LIBERO make it possible to compare task completion across policies (Liu et al., 2023). Deployment-oriented evaluation additionally needs to characterize responses to corrupted observations, incomplete instructions, and delayed commands. These deviations enter different parts of the closed loop, making their location and structure central to understanding policy robustness. Robustness benchmarks increasingly examine environmental, linguistic, and visual variation, as well as uncertainty across multiple interfaces (Pumacay et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2025; Fei et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2026; Guo et al., 2026). Their findings motivate a more specific question: which intervention sites and perturbation types account for a model’s loss of task success, and how does that loss change with intervention strength? Answering this question requires both broad coverage and a common evaluation protocol. An overall score alone can obscure failures concentrated in one input or control channel. We introduce LIBERO-MOR , a controlled stress-testing layer on four standard LIBERO suites. Its 22 perturbations cover vision, language, proprioception, action, and environment (Figure 1). A fixed manifest specifies three severity levels and five seeds for each of 40 tasks. Each perturbed episode is linked to a clean task–seed reference, allowing the same protocol to support overall comparisons, domain profiles, perturbation-specific fingerprints, and severity analysis.

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 VISION -L ANGUAGE -A CTION MODELS AND EVALUATION 

VLA designs build on scalable transformer control and vision-language transfer (Brohan et al., 2022; 2023), extending to open generalist policies (Kim et al., 2024; Team et al., 2024), flow-matching action experts (Black et al., 2024; 2025; Bjorck et al., 2025), diffusion action modules (Li et al., 2024a), latent-action representations (Bu et al., 2025), efficient continuous action chunking (Kim et al., 2025), active visual attention (Xiao et al., 2026), and discrete temporal block diffusion (Lee et al., 2026). Our evaluation spans this diversity of policy families and action-generation mechanisms. CALVIN, LIBERO, SIMPLER, RoboCasa, RoboTwin, and VLABench address long-horizon control, transfer, simulated policy evaluation, and task diversity (Mees et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024b; Nasiriany et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b). LIBERO-MOR complements these capability evaluations by measuring changes relative to each policy’s clean performance under the same harness. 22.2 ROBUSTNESS BENCHMARKS FOR ROBOT POLICIES 

The Colosseum, RoboTwin, LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO-Plus, and VLA-Arena study environmental variation, domain randomization, and structured robustness tests (Pumacay et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025; Fei et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025a). LIBERO-VPro focuses on closed-loop visual evidence failures (Li et al., 2026). The closest multimodal precedent is Guo et al. (2026), who evaluate 17 perturbations across action, observation, environment, and instruction uncertainty and introduce a robustness-training method. RoVLA, ProGAL-VLA, and ACoT-VLA further explore training and reasoning strategies for robust control (Luo et al., 2026; Darabi & Trivedi, 2026; Zhong et al., 2026). Building on prior multimodal stress tests, LIBERO-MOR explicitly separates proprioception from vision and evaluates 22 perturbations over the same task–seed grid at three intervention strengths. Its contribution lies in the shared diagnostic protocol and cross-model analysis: domain averages identify broad weaknesses, perturbation fingerprints reveal localized failures, and severity curves characterize how those failures develop. 

## 3 LIBERO-MOR B ENCHMARK 

LIBERO-MOR is a controlled stress-testing layer on standard LIBERO: the underlying task goal and success criterion are preserved while perturbations are injected at specific points of the closed-loop VLA pipeline (Figure 1, Table 1). 3.1 BENCHMARK DESIGN PRINCIPLES 

Deployment relevance. Perturbations represent deployment-facing failures in sensing, instruction, state estimation, control, and the physical scene. Their controlled formulation makes these factors reproducible and supports targeted analysis of policy responses. 

Pipeline coverage. We organize failures by where they enter the closed loop: vision , language ,

proprioception , action , and environment . The first three modify policy inputs, action perturbations modify commands before execution, and environment perturbations modify the scene or dynamics. 

Controlled single-factor interventions. Each perturbed rollout activates one perturbation at one severity while retaining the matched task, seed, goal, and original success checker. This design asso-ciates the measured response with a specific injected factor and captures its downstream consequences throughout the closed loop, providing a clear basis for comparing failure sources. 

Graded severity. Each perturbation has three ordered intervention strengths s ∈ { 1, 2, 3}. We measure the resulting success rates at every level, using the curves to characterize how closed-loop behavior changes with intervention strength. 3.2 MULTIMODAL PERTURBATION TAXONOMY 

The taxonomy is deliberately interface-centric: observation-side perturbations act before inference, action perturbations act between prediction and execution, and environment perturbations modify simulator state or scene. Domain labels follow the intervention site, which gives a consistent taxonomy even when effects propagate across interfaces. For example, an environment intervention changes the simulated scene and can subsequently alter visual observations. A vision intervention instead operates on the observation channel. Language under-specification changes the information presented to the policy while retaining the original benchmark goal and success checker. This separation between the task objective and the evidence available to the policy enables targeted evaluation of instruction robustness. 3.3 EVALUATION PROTOCOL AND METRICS

4.2 IMPLEMENTATION AND EVALUATION PROTOCOL 

All models share the standard LIBERO simulator (Liu et al., 2023), fixed manifest, 220-step episode cap, and action horizon of 10 environment steps. Model-specific adapters map the common interface to native preprocessing, state/action normalization, and output formats while preserving the shared tasks, perturbation operators, dynamics, and success criteria. Manifest audit confirms 200 clean and 13,200 perturbed episodes per model, 200 unique clean keys, 13,200 unique variant IDs, and zero invalid episodes. Execution validity verifies completion of the evaluation protocol, and the original LIBERO checker separately determines task success. This setup evaluates complete checkpoint–adapter systems under a common stress test. Native preprocessing and normalization connect simulator-side interventions to each policy’s representation and action interface. The resulting system-level profiles identify targets for follow-up ablations: concentrated proprioceptive weaknesses motivate testing state inputs, while language-specific failures motivate examining instruction processing under matched task conditions.

Domain averages can still mask sharply different failures within the same interface. Figure 5 therefore represents each model by a 22-dimensional perturbation-level RR fingerprint. The clearest example occurs in the language domain. OpenVLA-OFT retains 64.3–66.8% RR under paraphrase, ambiguous instruction, and typo corruption, yet collapses to 0.4% under distractor instruction. AVA-VLA exhibits the same qualitative pattern: 56.7–65.3% RR for the other language perturbations but only 1.4% for distractor instruction. By contrast, several other models remain above 60% RR under the same distractor perturbation. The contrast identifies distractor-instruction processing as a specific diagnostic target for examining instruction formatting, attention, and training coverage. Fine-grained visual and proprioceptive responses are similarly non-uniform. For π0.5, brightness and shadow each retain 76.8% RR, whereas motion and defocus blur fall to 34.4% and 34.2%. Severity analysis examines how vulnerabilities change as the intervention setting increases. Figure 6 traces model-normalized RR across three levels for four representative perturbations chosen to expose distinct response shapes. 

Progressive collapse versus a comparatively flat response. Motion blur produces sharply different trajectories. OpenVLA-OFT falls from 60.5% RR at mild severity to 28.6% at medium and 2.2% at strong severity; π0.5 follows a similar 67.0% → 35.6% → 0.5% trajectory. TBD-VLA instead remains near 68% across the three levels, while CogACT and GR00T-N1.7 decline less steeply. The approximately flat TBD-VLA curve separates the initial clean-to-perturbed loss from the subsequent response to stronger blur. Missing proprioception yields a related split. OpenVLA-OFT, AVA-VLA, and TBD-VLA reach 7.0%, 5.3%, and 0.5% RR at the strongest level, whereas π0.5, UniVLA, GR00T-N1.7, and CogACT remain approximately stable or show small increases. The strongest setting is thus a particularly informative stress test for the first three policies. 

Failure at the mildest setting versus graded degradation. OpenVLA-OFT and AVA-VLA already have only 0.5% and 1.6% RR under the mildest distractor instruction and remain near zero at higher levels. The other five models stay around 60–73%. Thus, the tested distractor format is sufficient to trigger failure in the first two systems without requiring the strongest setting. In contrast, all seven models lose RR as action delay increases, reaching 15.7–44.5% at strong severity. This shared downward trend identifies delayed execution as a broadly challenging control condition, with model-dependent rates of degradation. 

Interpreting non-monotonic responses. Each severity point summarizes 200 episodes over the same 40 tasks and five seeds. At this sample size, one changed outcome corresponds to 0.5 percentage points of SR before clean-rate normalization. Severity indexes the intervention setting, while empirical success captures the policy’s resulting closed-loop response. Small reversals can arise from finite-sample variation, task-specific interactions, or incidental benefits along a trajectory; stronger interventions can also change the distribution of visited states. Paired episode traces and operator-level checks provide a direct route to distinguishing these candidate explanations and locating the tasks or execution stages responsible for a reversal. 86 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 

6.1 IMPLICATIONS FOR ROBUSTNESS EVALUATION 

The results support a sequence of reporting decisions. First, report clean and perturbed success under the same execution protocol, so that the absolute loss remains visible. Second, provide RR as a complementary normalization, while retaining its clean denominator. Third, inspect domain and perturbation profiles before selecting a mitigation target. Finally, examine severity curves to distinguish failures already present at the mildest setting from those that worsen across the tested range. The same hierarchy supports evaluating robustness interventions: an improvement can be traced to its affected perturbations and severity levels, while the remaining domains reveal whether the benefit transfers across the pipeline. The choice of aggregation also matters for deployment-oriented interpretation. LIBERO-MOR uses uniform perturbation-type weights to establish a transparent and reproducible comparison distribution. A deployment with frequent communication delay may prioritize a different profile from one dominated by poor visual conditions. Accordingly, the overall benchmark score summarizes a specified test mixture, while the disaggregated results allow readers to examine the conditions relevant to their application. Aligning operators, severity ranges, checkpoints, and rollout budgets provides a common basis for extending these comparisons to other robustness benchmarks. 6.2 SCOPE AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS 

LIBERO-MOR establishes a controlled evaluation setting within the LIBERO embodiment, using family-specific severity scales, single-factor interventions, binary task success, and five seeds per task. The shared execution budget supports systematic comparisons and several directions for extending the benchmark. Recovery time, trajectory quality, and partial progress can enrich the behavioral characterization; expanded seed coverage and episode-level uncertainty estimates can sharpen task-specific comparisons. Controlled ablations of architecture, pretraining, fine-tuning, and adapters can further explain the observed system-level differences. Additional embodiments, compound interventions, policy-native execution settings, and real-robot experiments can test how these robustness profiles transfer across deployment conditions. 

Conclusion. LIBERO-MOR evaluates perturbations across the VLA control loop with a matched, multi-severity protocol. Across seven policies, substantial aggregate losses coexist with distinct perturbation-specific and severity-dependent responses. These results make the case for reporting
