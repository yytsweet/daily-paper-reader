## 摘要
动作分词器使机器人策略能够将控制视为自回归的下一词元预测。随着这些策略在更大、更多样的数据集上训练，它们将需要一个通用分词器，能够编码来自多种具身和数据源的动作，包括未见过的具身和数据源。我们研究大规模动作分词，在来自真实机器人、手持夹爪、仿真和人类的 4.9 万小时动作数据上进行训练。我们提出神经动作分词器（NAT），这是一种因果分词器，其解码器允许在生成词元的同时执行动作。在四个仿真基准上，NAT 在对未见过的机器人具身进行分词时优于 FAST 和先前的神经动作分词器；在视觉-语言-动作模型中，当其词元驱动动作时，NAT 在四个基准和真实机器人上优于 FAST+。NAT 词元还在部分基准上缩小了与流匹配动作头的性能差距，同时降低了首批动作的推理延迟。我们的实验还发现，学习有效动作空间可能并不需要机器人数据：仅用人类手部运动训练的 NAT 性能接近用机器人数据训练的分词器。

## Abstract
Action tokenizers let robot policies treat control as autoregressive next-token prediction. As these policies are trained on larger and more diverse datasets, they will need a universal tokenizer that can encode actions from many embodiments and data sources, including ones it has not seen. We study action tokenization at scale, training on 49k hours of action data from real-world robots, handheld grippers, simulation, and humans. We introduce Neural Action Tokenizer (NAT), a causal tokenizer whose decoder lets actions be executed as their tokens are generated. Across four simulation benchmarks, NAT outperforms FAST and prior neural action tokenizers when tokenizing unseen robot embodiments, and in vision-language-action models it outperforms FAST+ across four benchmarks and on a real robot when acting through its tokens. NAT tokens also close the performance gap to a flow-matching action head on some benchmarks while lowering the inference latency for first actions. Our experiments also find that robot data may not be necessary to learn an effective action space: a NAT trained only on human hand motion performs close to tokenizers trained on robot data.
