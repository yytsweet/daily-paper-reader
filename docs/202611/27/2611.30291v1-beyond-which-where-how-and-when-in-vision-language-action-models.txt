# Beyond Which: Where, How, and When in Vision-Language-Action Models

## Abstract 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models typically rely on vision-language model (VLM) features as the primary per-ceptual source for action prediction, showing strong gen-eralization across diverse robotic manipulation tasks. Al-though VLM features can identify which entities are relevant to action prediction, precise manipulation also requires un-derstanding where task-relevant entities are located within the spatial layout, how their geometric structure constrains physical interaction, and when spatial or geometric infor-mation should make a greater contribution to action predic-tion. To this end, we introduce W3H-VLA to organize VLA perception and action prediction around which, where, how, and when. Specifically, W3H-VLA retains VLM-based semantic entity perception (which) and further introduces spatial distance perception (where) and geometric structure perception (how) with adaptive spatial-geometric guidance (when) to enrich the perceptual information available for action prediction. Experiments across real-world settings and simulation benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and superior performance of W3H-VLA .

## 1. Introduction 

Robot policies optimized for specific tasks often suffer from limited generalization [13]. Meanwhile, recent advances in large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) have demon-strated strong capabilities in multi-modal learning and rea-soning [1, 2]. The advances provide a strong basis for vi-sual perception and language understanding, enabling robot policies to leverage broad vision-language knowledge for interpreting visual observations and language instructions across the physical world. Building upon these, vision-language-action (VLA) models combine a pre-trained VLM for perceptual and semantic understanding with an action prediction module that maps observations and instructions to robot actions, enabling robot policies to generalize across diverse tasks [3, 11, 13, 20, 40, 45]. Consider the task of placing a blue cube surrounded by other cubes onto a cube tower. W3H-VLA introduces spatial distance perception by in-corporating task-relevant distance relationships and spatial layout features derived from a pre-trained depth estima-tion model [35]. About how , W3H-VLA enables geo-metric structure perception by capturing task-relevant ge-ometric structure features from a pre-trained visual geom-etry model [31]. For when , W3H-VLA integrates adap-tive spatial-geometric guidance into action prediction by co-ordinating the contributions of spatial distance perception and geometric structure perception, conditioned on the cur-rent task semantic context and evolving action state. By unifying which , where , how , and when , W3H-VLA com-plements semantic entity perception with spatial distance perception, geometric structure perception, and adaptive spatial-geometric guidance to support precise action predic-tion in the complex physical world. To validate the effectiveness of W3H-VLA, we conduct extensive experiments on 4 benchmarks, LIBERO [17], LIBERO-Plus [6], CALVIN [21], LIBERO-PRO [44], and 4 real-world manipulation tasks. The contributions of this work are summarized as follows. • We formulate VLA perception and action prediction in terms of which , where , how , and when , extending VLM-based semantic entity perception with spatial distance perception, geometric structure perception, and adaptive spatial-geometric guidance. • Building on the formulation and the corresponding per-ceptual design, we propose W3H-VLA, a novel VLA framework that incorporates a broader range of percep-tual information into action prediction. • We evaluate W3H-VLA on 4 benchmarks and 4 real-world manipulation tasks, showing its effectiveness and superior performance in simulated and physical settings. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Vision-Language-Action Models 

VLA models build upon pre-trained VLMs to map vi-sual observations and language instructions to robot ac-tions [3, 20, 25, 40]. One line of research advances VLA models by scaling robot interaction data and vision-language pre-training. RT-1 [4] demonstrates the benefits of large and diverse real-world robot data, and RT-2 [45] transfers web-scale vision-language knowledge to action prediction. Open X-Embodiment [22] aggregates cross-embodiment robot data, enabling the training of generalist policies [13, 30]. More recently, π0.5 [11] integrates het-erogeneous robot and web data to improve open-world gen-eralization. Another line of research improves VLA action prediction through specialized policy architectures. RDT-1B [18] employs the diffusion model for continuous action prediction. Within the flow matching paradigm, π0 [3] in-troduces an action expert for continuous action chunks, X-VLA [40] incorporates soft prompts for cross-embodiment learning, and SimVLA [20] adopts a lightweight action transformer. 

2.2. Perception for Robotic Manipulation 

Research on robotic perception has progressively expanded from 2D semantic perception to spatial and geometric rep-resentations, and more recently to adaptive perceptual guid-ance. Early methods primarily extract semantic features from 2D visual observations. CLIPort [27] combines VLM-derived semantic features with spatially precise action pre-diction, and RoboGround [10] predicts masks for relevant entities and placement regions to guide manipulation. Be-yond 2D semantics, subsequent methods incorporate depth and 3D representations to capture physical structure fea-tures. PerAct [28] encodes depth observations into a voxel grid, Act3D [8] uses depth observations to lift pre-trained 2D features into a 3D field, and RVT [9] aggregates fea-tures across virtual views for 3D-aware action prediction. Recent studies further explore adaptive perceptual guidance conditioned on task demands and action execution context. HyperTASR [29] adaptively modulates scene features ac-cording to task and execution progress, and ActiveVLA [19] dynamically selects 3D regions, viewpoints, and spatial res-olutions for precise manipulation. In contrast, W3H-VLA unifies VLM-based semantic entity perception to identify which entities are relevant to action prediction, spatial distance perception to establish 

where relevant entities are located, geometric structure per-ception to capture how the geometric details constrain phys-ical interaction, and adaptive spatial-geometric guidance to determine when the spatial distance or geometric structure perception should make a greater contribution to precise ac-tion prediction.

3.1. Preliminary 

Problem formulation. At time step t, given the 2D visual observation It and a language instruction L, the robot policy πθ predicts a continuous action chunk At =[at, at+1 , . . . , at+H−1] over an action horizon of H steps. 

At = πθ (It, L) , (1) Although VLA models have shown generalization rely-ing primarily on semantic entity perception derived from 2D visual observations, precise action prediction often requires additional forms of perception. Consequently, W3H-VLA organizes VLA perception and action prediction beyond semantic entity perception ( which ) by introducing spatial distance perception ( where ), geometric structure perception (how ), and adaptive spatial-geometric guidance ( when ). 

Base VLA model. W3H-VLA adopts π0.5 [11] as the base model. π0.5 comprises a pre-trained PaliGemma VLM [2] and an action expert. The VLM encodes the 2D visual observations and language instruction into vision-language features, which the action expert leverages to predict con-tinuous action chunks via conditional flow matching [16].

3.5. Training and Inference 

Training. W3H-VLA comprises a VLM, a spatial dis-tance perception branch equipped with a depth estimation model, a geometric structure perception branch equipped with a visual geometry model, and an action expert in-corporating adaptive spatial-geometric guidance. During training, the pre-trained VLM, depth estimation model, and visual geometry model remain frozen, with optimization of the action expert and all new parameters introduced by 

W3H-VLA. All trainable parameters are jointly optimized using the original conditional flow-matching objective of the base VLA model, without dedicated objectives for spa-tial distance or geometric structure perception. 

Inference. During inference, the VLM encodes the 2D vi-sual observation and language instruction to produce the fi-nal vision-language feature and vision-language key-value cache. In parallel, the depth estimation model and visual geometry model process the same observation to extract the spatial distance and geometric structure features, respec-tively. Starting from Gaussian noise, the action expert it-eratively generates an action chunk by interacting with the vision-language key-value cache and incorporating spatial distance and geometric structure perception from the corre-sponding key-value banks. This process extends the VLA perception to support precise action prediction.

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Simulation benchmarks. W3H-VLA is evaluated across 4 benchmarks following the respective evaluation protocols. • LIBERO. LIBERO [17] comprises 4 task suites, i.e. ,LIBERO-Spatial (Spatial), LIBERO-Object (Object), LIBERO-Goal (Goal), and LIBERO-Long (Long), each containing 10 manipulation tasks. Following the standard LIBERO protocol, the policy is trained on 50 human-teleoperated demonstrations and evaluated over 50 tri-als per task, totaling 2,000 evaluation trials across the 4 suites. The results are reported as the task success rate for each suite and the average across all 4 suites. • LIBERO-Plus. LIBERO-Plus [6] extends LIBERO to 10,030 evaluation episodes for evaluating policy robust-ness under 7 perturbation types, i.e. , camera viewpoint (Camera), robot initial state (Robot), language instruc-tion (Language), lighting condition (Light), background texture (Background), sensor noise (Noise), and object layout (Layout). For evaluation, the policy trained on LIBERO is directly tested on LIBERO-Plus. The results are reported as the task success rate for each type of per-turbation and the overall across all 7 dimensions. • CALVIN. CALVIN [21] comprises 34 language-guided manipulation tasks across 4 diverse environments, A, B, C, and D. Following the ABC →D protocol, the policy is trained on A, B, and C and evaluated on D. The policy is evaluated on 1,000 task sequences, each comprising 5 consecutive manipulation tasks. The results are reported as the average length of consecutively completed tasks per sequence. The task success rates at each sequence length are provided in the appendix. • LIBERO-PRO. LIBERO-PRO [44] extends 4 LIBERO task suites with perturbations, i.e. , object attributes, ini-tial spatial configurations, semantic instruction variations, and task goals. Following the standard LIBERO-PRO evaluation protocol, the policy trained on LIBERO is di-rectly tested on LIBERO-PRO. The overall task success rate is reported, with detailed results provided in the ap-pendix. 

Real-world experiments. We further evaluate W3H-VLA through real-world experiments on the AgileX Piper robot. Specifically, 4 tasks are designed to evaluate semantic en-tity perception ( which ), spatial distance perception ( where ), geometric structure perception ( how ), and adaptive spatial-geometric guidance ( when ), respectively. For each task, policies are trained on 50 human-teleoperated demonstra-tions and evaluated on 20 trials. A trial is successful only if the full manipulation is completed and the task-specific goal is achieved. The success rate for each task and the average across all 4 tasks are reported. • Which. Given various geometric blocks, the robot is in-structed to pick and place the specified one on the tar-get plate. During testing, the block positions are varied across trials, thereby evaluating semantic entity percep-tion for identifying which entities are relevant to action prediction. • Where. Together, these designs enrich VLA perception with spatial and geometric information for precise action prediction in the complex physical world.
