# Abstract 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models integrate visual per-ception and language understanding from vision-language models (VLMs) with action prediction for robotic manipu-lation. Although multi-view observations provide comple-mentary scene information, VLMs model cross-view inter-actions based on 2D feature similarity, which can lead to physical inconsistency in the form of cross-view incorrect association between distinct entities and cross-view infor-mation fragmentation about the same entity. To address these challenges, we propose ConsisX , a novel VLA frame-work that integrates cross-view association and entity co-herence to enable cross-view consistency thinking in 3D for physically consistent robotic manipulation. Specifically, Cross-View Association Modeling (CAM) is proposed to in-troduce cross-view spatial proximity constraints in a shared 3D space to reduce cross-view incorrect association. En-tity Coherence Organization (ECO) is designed to organize cross-view local information into coherent 3D entity evi-dence, thereby addressing cross-view information fragmen-tation. Consistent Action Conditioning (CAC) is introduced to condition action prediction on task-relevant 3D entity ev-idence for cross-view consistent action execution. Experi-ments on 3 real-world manipulation tasks and 4 simulation benchmarks establish ConsisX as an effective and competi-tive framework for robotic manipulation. 

# 1. Introduction 

In modern embodied intelligence systems, robots take natural language instructions and multi-view visual ob-servations as input to predict actions for task execu-tion. Recently, vision-language-action (VLA) models have advanced robotic manipulation by extending the visual and language understanding of pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) [1, 2] to robot control through an action 1arXiv:2611.30308v1 [cs.RO] 23 Nov 2026 prediction module optimized via demonstration learning [31, 39]. RT-2 and OpenVLA adapt VLMs into robotic policies to predict actions as discrete tokens [15, 44]. Sub-sequently, π0 introduces an action expert to predict contin-uous action sequences [3]. Building upon the π0, π0.5 com-bines diverse robot and vision-language data during training for enhanced generalization to unseen environments [12]. A common setup for robotic policies incorporates multi-view visual inputs from environment and wrist cameras to provide complementary observations of the current scene for reliable action prediction. To integrate these obser-vations, VLA models enable interactions between visual features from different views through jointly processing within the VLM. However, the joint processing can intro-duce cross-view ambiguity. As shown in Fig. 1(a), both cups appear in the environment view but only one is visible in the wrist view, and feature interactions may link the front one to the rear one. In Fig. 1(b), the two cameras capture different faces of the same triangular prism, and 2D feature similarity alone may fail to link the local observations. The above cases highlight a challenge: cross-view inter-actions in VLMs rely on learned similarities between 2D features and can therefore lead to physically inconsistent action execution. Specifically, the challenge involves two forms of cross-view ambiguity. (Q1) Visually similar ob-servations may be incorrectly associated despite corre-sponding to distinct physical entities. (Q2) Cross-view information remains fragmented despite coming from the same physical entity. 

Do visually similar observations across views actually refer to the same physical entity? How should fragmented information from different views of the same physical entity be organized? We regard these ambiguities as a 3D cross-view consistency problem and argue that cross-view think-ing should jointly consider cross-view association and en-tity coherence in 3D. Specifically, visual similarity alone is insufficient to establish physical identity, and cross-view association is therefore needed to provide 3D spatial con-straints. The fragmented information from the same entity also requires entity coherence to guide its organization into physically consistent evidence. The above considerations lead to the central question: How to think about cross-view association and entity coherence to enable cross-view con-sistency in 3D? 

To answer this question, we propose ConsisX, a novel VLA framework that combines cross-view association and entity coherence for cross-view consistency thinking in 3D. Specifically, the consistency thinking follows a progressive process. Firstly, Cross-View Association Modeling (CAM) 

is proposed to organize 3D features from each view in a shared 3D coordinate system and infer cross-view associa-tion with spatial proximity constraints and feature similar-ity. (A1) CAM provides cross-view correspondences un-der 3D spatial constraint, thereby reducing cross-view incorrect association. Secondly, Entity Coherence Orga-nization (ECO) groups fragmented local 3D feature within each view into entity candidates through spatial affinity and feature compatibility before organizing cross-view entity pairs according to the cross-view association from CAM. 

(A2) ECO organizes cross-view local information into coherent 3D entity evidence, thus addressing cross-view information fragmentation. Finally, Consistent Action Conditioning (CAC) retrieves candidate entity pairs from ECO according to task relevance, providing the correspond-ing 3D entity features for cross-view consistent action pre-diction. ConsisX thus jointly considers cross-view associa-tion and entity coherence, enabling cross-view consistency thinking in 3D for reliable robotic manipulation. The main contributions of this work can be summarized as follows. • To address the challenge of cross-view physical inconsis-tency, we propose ConsisX, a novel VLA framework that integrates cross-view association and entity coherence to enable cross-view consistency thinking in 3D. • We design CAM to reduce cross-view incorrect associ-ations through 3D spatial constraint, ECO to organize fragmented local information into coherent 3D entity ev-idence, and CAC to provide task relevant 3D entity fea-tures for cross-view consistent action prediction. • We evaluate ConsisX on 4 simulation benchmarks and conduct real-world experiments on 3 manipulation tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness and competitive perfor-mance of the proposed framework. 

# 2. Related Work 

2.1. Vision-Language-Action Models 

VLA models integrate visual perception, language under-standing, and action prediction for robotic manipulation [3, 12, 15]. Recent progress builds on scalable robot learning and pre-trained vision language knowledge. RT-1 [4] and Octo [29] learn from diverse robot demonstra-tions, while RT-2 [44] and OpenVLA [15] adapt pre-trained VLMs. To further advance VLA models, some studies ex-plore ways to improve policy adaptation and generaliza-tion. OpenVLA-OFT [16] improves adaptation with op-timized fine-tuning, and π0.5 [12] enhances generalization through more diverse data. For action prediction, FAST [23] compresses action sequences into discrete tokens, and 

π0 [3] introduces flow matching for continuous action pre-diction. Beyond direct action prediction, some methods in-tegrate intermediate reasoning to improve action prediction [35, 38, 41].

2.3. 3D-Aware Policy Learning 

3D-aware learning improves spatial understanding in robotic policies for reliable action execution in the physi-cal world. Some approaches incorporate additional spatial information into policy learning. PerAct [28] predicts ac-tions from voxelized RGB-D observations, and RVT [10] aggregates point cloud-based virtual views. For VLA mod-els, 4D-VLA [36] augments visual feature with 3D position encoding, and PointACT [6] introduces point cloud feature for action prediction. Researchers further explore 3D object keypoint constraints [19] and 3D-consistent waypoints [11] for robot control. Other VLA methods leverage pre-trained 3D vision models to refine visual features [9, 17]. In contrast, ConsisX integrates cross-view association and entity coherence to enable cross-view consistency thinking in 3D. Through spatially constrained cross-view association and coherent entity organization, ConsisX lever-ages cross-view 3D entity evidence for physically consis-tent action prediction. 

# 3. Method 

This section details how ConsisX enables cross-view con-sistency thinking in 3D. Sec. 3.1 introduces the problem formulation and the base policy. We then detail CAM in Sec. 3.2, ECO in Sec. 3.3, and CAC in Sec. 3.4. The train-ing and inference are finally described in Sec. 3.5. 

3.1. Preliminary 

Problem formulation. Given a language instruction ℓ and multi-view visual observations Ot = {IEt , I Wt } from an environment camera and a wrist camera at time t, the pol-icy πθ predicts the next H continuous actions as an action chunk At = [ at, at+1 , . . . , at+H−1]. The process can be expressed as follows. 

πθ : ( Ot, ℓ ) → At. (1) 

Base policy. ConsisX is built on π0.5 [12], a base policy that combines a pre-trained VLM [2] with a flow-matching action expert [20]. The VLM jointly processes the current multi-view visual observations and the language instruc-tion embeddings to produce layer-wise vision-language fea-tures. The action expert is conditioned on these layer-wise features and iteratively refines a noisy action sequence to produce the final action chunk. In the base policy, cross-view interactions within the VLM rely on 2D visual feature similarity and may lead to physical inconsistency in the form of cross-view incorrect association between distinct entities and cross-view infor-mation fragmentation about the same entity. ConsisX there-3fore introduces CAM, ECO, and CAC as a progressive pro-cess for cross-view consistency thinking in 3D.

3.5. Training and Inference 

Training. ConsisX integrates the proposed CAM, ECO, and CAC modules with a pre-trained VLM, an action ex-pert, and a pre-trained 3D vision model. During training, the parameters of the action expert and the proposed mod-ules are optimized, with the pre-trained VLM and 3D vi-sion model kept frozen. All of the trainable parameters are jointly updated using the flow-matching objective of the base policy [12]. 

Inference. ConsisX takes the multi-view observations and language instruction as input and employs the VLM for joint encoding and the 3D vision model for 3D feature ex-traction. Subsequently, CAM establishes cross-view asso-ciation to support ECO in maintaining entity coherence, enabling CAC to retrieve task-relevant 3D entity features. Conditioned on 3D entity evidence, the action expert itera-tively transforms Gaussian noise into an action chunk.

# 5. Conclusion 

Existing VLA models rely on 2D feature similarity within VLMs to model cross-view interactions, which can lead to physical inconsistency in the form of incorrect cross-view association and cross-view information fragmentation. To address these challenges, we propose ConsisX, a novel VLA framework that integrates cross-view association and 8entity coherence to enable cross-view consistency thinking in 3D. ConsisX implements the consistency thinking fol-lowing a progressive process, with CAM establishing spa-tially constrained cross-view associations, ECO organizing local 3D features into cross-view coherent entity pairs, and CAC conditioning action prediction on task-relevant 3D en-tity evidence. Evaluations on 4 simulation benchmarks and 3 real-world tasks highlight the value of cross-view consis-tency thinking in 3D for reliable robotic manipulation.
