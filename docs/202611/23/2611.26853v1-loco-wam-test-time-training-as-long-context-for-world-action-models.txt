LoCo-WAM: Test-Time Training as Long Context for World-Action Models
Sheng Cheng1
Deqian Kong2
1Amazon AGI
2University of California, Los Angeles
Abstract
World-action models guide robot actions by predicting what
happens next, but typically condition on a short observation
history. Extending this history increases computation at ev-
ery decision and can require a separately trained model.
We introduce LoCo-WAM, which learns from the outcomes
of its actions through test-time training while keeping the
same short history length. It uses newly observed frames
to update a low-rank adapter in the video expert, whose
adapted predictions guide subsequent actions.
The up-
dates accumulate within each episode and are reset between
episodes, allowing past observations to influence decisions
after they leave the input window. LoCo-WAM thus learns
from a longer stream of experience without expert action la-
bels, additional data or meta-training. Experiments on two
world-action models show improved task success across
four simulation benchmarks. On RoboCasa GR-1, a model
with 2.4 s of history matches one trained with 9.6 s, while a
model with 4.8 s exceeds it by 4.8 percentage points. Our
analysis shows that the updates improve prediction of up-
coming frames, affect the action mainly through adaptation
of the imagined future, and have an increasing effect on ac-
tions as the episode unfolds.
1. Introduction
Human behavior combines knowledge acquired over time
with rapid learning from new experience, as described by
complementary learning systems theory [35]. In motor con-
trol, discrepancies between predicted and observed sensory
outcomes can drive adaptation during action [43]. These
findings motivate learning during robot execution, where
the outcomes of actions provide feedback for the predic-
tions that guide subsequent behavior [37].
World-action models [13, 21, 24, 30, 31] provide a nat-
ural setting for such learning. They predict future frames
together with actions, drawing on video pretraining to learn
how scenes evolve. In the models we study, the imagined
future guides action generation. After the robot acts, the
observed frames provide targets for the model’s own video
loss. As interactions within a task share objects, surround-

This learning allows experience to influence behavior be-
yond a fixed observation window. Most world-action mod-
els condition on a short history, which limits the past ob-
servations available at each decision. Extending the his-
tory [21, 30] increases computation and can require a sep-
arately trained model. Selecting or compressing the his-
tory requires components that are trained together with the
policy [46, 49, 54]. Updating the model on the observed
frames provides another way to use the past observations.
These updates are a form of test-time training, a family of
methods that update a model on each test input with a self-
supervised loss [40], which has been extended to language
and video generation models [12, 17, 41, 42, 50].
We introduce LoCo-WAM, a test-time training method
for world-action models, which trains the video expert dur-
ing execution with its video loss on the observed frames.
At every decision, LoCo-WAM takes one gradient step on
a low-rank adapter [18] of the video expert to predict the
most recently observed frames from the history that pre-

The action expert then generates the next action from the

We evaluate LoCo-WAM on two world-action models,
Long-WAM [21] and LingBot-VA [30], and four simula-
tion benchmarks: RoboCasa GR-1 [4], RoboCasa365 [36],
LIBERO-Mem [11] and RoboTwin 2.0 [8]. LoCo-WAM
improves the base model on all four benchmarks.
On
RoboCasa GR-1, Long-WAM with 2.4 s of history reaches
73.6% with LoCo-WAM, on par with the base model trained
with 9.6 s of history, at 0.76 s per decision compared with
0.67 s and without training a separate model. With 4.8 s
of history, it reaches 78.3%, 4.8 points above the 9.6 s
model (Fig. 1). On LIBERO-Mem, LingBot-VA with an
8-chunk history matches its default 30-chunk history. On
both benchmarks, LoCo-WAM also outperforms test-time
methods for robot policies, including AdaWorldPolicy [55],
which adapts the world model across the episodes of a task,
and RoboTTT [23], which meta-trains fast-weight layers.
Our analysis on RoboCasa GR-1 examines what an up-
date of the video expert learns from the observed frames,
how it reaches the action, and how its effect develops over
the episode. The update improves the prediction of upcom-
ing frames before they are used for training, with larger
reductions in video loss for shorter histories.
Using the
model’s own predictions as targets in place of the observed
frames removes the gain in success rate. The update af-
fects the action mainly through the imagined future, since
applying it only when the video expert predicts this future
recovers most of the gain. We further show that updating
only the video expert performs better than also updating the
action expert with the action loss. Over the episode, the
change of the action accumulates, and stopping the updates
early removes most of the gain.
In summary, our contributions are:
• We introduce LoCo-WAM, test-time training as long con-
text for world-action models: the video expert is trained
during execution with its video loss on the observed
frames, without labels, additional data or meta-training.
• On two world-action models and four simulation bench-
marks, LoCo-WAM improves the base model on all four
benchmarks, enables a model with a short history to
match or exceed the base model with a longer history, and
outperforms prior test-time methods for robot policies.
• We analyze how the update of the video expert reaches
the action: it improves the prediction of upcoming frames
from the observed frames, affects the action mainly
through the imagined future, and the change of the action
it causes accumulates over the episode.
2. Related Work
World-action models.
World-action models predict fu-
ture frames together with actions, which enables them to
benefit from large-scale video pretraining and to use the pre-
dicted future to choose actions [9, 13, 26, 27, 37]. Exam-
ples include UniPi [13], GR-1 [52], VPP [19], UVA [31],
UWM [62], Cosmos Policy [24], OpenWAM [51], Fast-
WAM [56] and LingBot-VA [30], many of which pair a pre-
trained video model that predicts the future frames with an
action model that generates the actions from this prediction.
Many manipulation tasks are long-horizon, and the in-
formation needed to act, such as which steps have already
been completed, is often no longer visible in the current ob-
servation, so a policy has to rely on its history. Most world-
action models, however, condition on a single frame or a
short history. Some methods train the model with longer
histories, with a separate model for each history length in
Long-WAM [21] and with long video-action sequences in
LingBot-VA [30]. Others select or compress the history,
with a downsampled history stream in HiLo-WAM [49], an-
chor frames and gist tokens in MemoryWAM [54], event
memory banks in DiM-WAM [46], and the first observa-
tion next to the recent history in ReCAP [53]. LoCo-WAM
keeps the short-history model and trains it at test time on the
observed frames, which enables it to reach the performance
of a longer history.
Test-time training.
Test-time training updates a model on
each test input with a self-supervised loss and was intro-
duced to handle distribution shift [15, 40, 45, 47]. For long
context, dynamic evaluation [28], Temp-LoRA [50], TTT-
E2E [42] and SlowFast-VGen [17] update the weights of
the model, or a temporary LoRA, on the context during in-
ference, and TTT layers [41], Titans [2], LaCT [59] and
One-Minute Video Generation [12] build such updates into
the architecture as fast-weight layers. LoCo-WAM belongs
to the first group and trains a low-rank adapter of a world-
action model with its own video loss. In a world-action
model, the self-supervised task, video prediction, shares the
video expert with the main task, the prediction of the action.
In robotics, PAD [16] adapts a visuomotor policy dur-
ing deployment with a self-supervised loss. AdaWorldPol-
icy [55], WorldAgen [44] and T3VF [38] adapt a world
model with its prediction loss, mainly to handle distribu-
tion shift, and SALT [29] adapts the action side under visual
disruptions. RoboTTT [23] and WAM-TTT [14] meta-train
fast-weight layers, to extend the context of a policy and to
learn from human videos, respectively. Test-time reinforce-
ment learning [1, 32] updates a policy with a reward signal.

3. Method
This section presents LoCo-WAM. We first describe how
a world-action model acts from a fixed history (Sec. 3.1).
We then introduce LoCo-WAM, which trains a world-action
model at test time on the current episode with its pretraining
losses (Sec. 3.2). Finally, we describe the setting used in our
experiments, which updates only the video expert, and how
the update reaches the action (Sec. 3.3).
3.1. World-action models with a fixed history

latents of the upcoming frames from the history, indepen-
dently of the action tokens of the current chunk, and the ac-
tion expert then samples the next action chunk conditioned
on the history and this prediction:

Asynchronous update. In the synchronous form, the Sv

4. Experiments
4.1. Setup
Evaluation benchmarks.
We apply LoCo-WAM to
Long-WAM [21] and LingBot-VA [30], two world-action
models that condition on a fixed history. We evaluate each
base model on two closed-loop simulation benchmarks un-
der the official protocols, run each setting three times, and
report the mean and standard deviation of the success rate
in percent.
RoboCasa GR-1. RoboCasa GR-1 [4] consists of 24
tabletop tasks for a GR-1 humanoid, 18 pick-and-place and
6 articulated tasks, whose demonstrations last 10 to 18 s on
average. Long-WAM provides models trained with 0, 2.4,
4.8 and 9.6 s of history, which serve as our short- and long-
context models, and each evaluation runs 10 trials per task.
RoboCasa365. RoboCasa365 [36] contains atomic and
composite household tasks in simulated kitchens. Follow-
ing Long-WAM, we evaluate its released model with 2.4 s
of history on the 50 target tasks with 50 trials per task.
LIBERO-Mem.
LIBERO-Mem [11] consists of 10
tabletop tasks in which a Franka arm repeatedly manipu-
lates the same objects, so that the progress of the task can-
not be inferred from the current observation. We fine-tune
LingBot-VA on its demonstrations and evaluate it on 20
held-out initial states per task with an 8-chunk history, using
the default 30-chunk history as the long-history reference,
and report the success rate and the subgoal completion rate.
RoboTwin 2.0. RoboTwin 2.0 [8] is a bimanual bench-
mark with 50 tasks, on which LingBot-VA with its default
72-chunk history already reaches 91.5% average success.
We therefore select the 8 tasks with the lowest success
rate and split them into the 4 tasks on which the 72-chunk
history clearly outperforms a 16-chunk history in the full-
benchmark evaluation and the remaining 4, with 100 trials
per task in scenes without domain randomization.
Baselines.
We compare LoCo-WAM with three kinds of
baselines, namely prior methods with the results reported in
the literature, the base model without test-time updates, and
test-time methods that we reimplement on the same base
models. The base model is evaluated both with the same
history as LoCo-WAM and with a longer history, up to 9.6 s
for Long-WAM and 30 and 72 chunks for LingBot-VA on
LIBERO-Mem and RoboTwin 2.0.
• AdaWorldPolicy [55] updates a LoRA with the prediction
loss of the world model and accumulates the adaptation
across the episodes of a task.
• RoboTTT [23] adds fast-weight layers to the action ex-
pert, meta-trains them on the benchmark demonstrations
and updates them at every decision. We also evaluate it
without the memory update.
• Future Consensus [39] samples several candidate futures
at every decision and executes the one closest to their
mean, which increases the inference cost by the number
of candidates.
• ReCAP [53] keeps the first observation as an anchor next
to the most recent history and thus extends the input of
the model.
Experiment setting.
Unless stated otherwise, LoCo-
WAM adds a LoRA of rank 32 to the self-attention and
feed-forward layers of the video expert and takes one up-
date step per decision, Sv = 1, with a learning rate that
depends on the base model and the benchmark.

4.3. Compute cost
LoCo-WAM substitutes for a longer history at a moderate
cost in latency. With the synchronous update, a decision
of LoCo-WAM with 2.4 s of history takes 0.76 s, compared
with 0.67 s for the base model with 9.6 s of history, whose
success rate it matches, as the latency column of Tab. 1
shows. The asynchronous update of Sec. 3.3 removes the
update from the time between observation and action, since
it runs while the robot executes the current action chunk.
With it, a decision takes 0.62 s with 2.4 s of history and
0.64 s with 4.8 s, less than the 0.67 s of the 9.6 s base model,
at success rates of 74.9% and 76.9%, the latter 3.4 points
above the 9.6 s base model. The update still runs at every
decision, so the compute per decision remains 0.81 s. LoCo-
WAM increases the peak GPU memory in Tab. 1 from 13.7
to 14.6 GB with 2.4 s of history, for the adapter and its back-
ward pass, compared with 14.0 GB for the base model with
9.6 s and 15.4 GB for AdaWorldPolicy. LoCo-WAM also
needs no additional offline training, whereas the 9.6 s model
is a separately trained model, and RoboTTT requires about
136 GPU-hours of meta-training and takes 1.06 s per deci-
sion.

5. Conclusion
We presented LoCo-WAM, which trains the video expert of
a world-action model at test time with its own video loss on
the observed frames, while keeping the architecture and the
history length of the model. On two world-action models
and four simulation benchmarks, LoCo-WAM improves the
base model on all of them, and on RoboCasa GR-1, a model
with 2.4 s of history matches the base model with 9.6 s, and
a model with 4.8 s exceeds it by 4.8 points. Our analy-
sis shows that the update learns from the observed frames
to predict upcoming frames, and that it affects the action
mainly through the imagined future. This change of the ac-
tion accumulates over the episode and depends on updating
throughout it. In a world-action model, the environment
provides the targets of the video loss at every decision, and
LoCo-WAM shows that training on these targets at test time
enables a short history to reach the performance of a longer
one.

B. Additional analysis
Resetting the adapter at every episode.
LoCo-WAM resets the adapter at the start of every episode, so that each episode
is adapted only to its own observed frames. A natural alternative keeps the adapter across the episodes of a task, as Ada-
WorldPolicy does with its world model. On LingBot-VA with LIBERO-Mem, this alternative lowers the success rate by 3.7
points and the subgoal completion rate by 4.0 points relative to the base model in the same runs, and all three runs fall below
the base model. The adaptation to one episode therefore does not carry over to later episodes, which is why LoCo-WAM
resets the adapter.
A larger step does not help the action update.
In Tab. 6, updating the action expert alone improves the base model less
than updating the video expert, and it also takes much smaller steps, changing the adapter by 0.002 in norm per update
compared with 0.059 and 0.035 for the video update with 2.4 and 4.8 s of history. When we scale its learning rate to match
these steps, the action update reaches 63.2% and 69.3% on RoboCasa GR-1, against 71.0% and 69.6% for the base model in
the same runs, and thus no longer improves the base model. The smaller step therefore does not account for the lower success
rate of the action update, which supports updating the video expert in LoCo-WAM.
