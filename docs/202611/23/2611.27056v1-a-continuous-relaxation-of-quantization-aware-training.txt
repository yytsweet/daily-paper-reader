ABSTRACT
Quantization is a critical piece of the deployment pipeline in the era of large generative models. Low-bit
quantization, particularly 2-bits or sub-2-bits, is a notoriously difficult problem, particularly when using a
channelwise quantization scheme which has been shown as a leading candidate for hardware-efficiency. The key
challenges with low-bit quantization range from limited quantization levels to saddle points or flat regions in the
loss surface. In this paper, we look at the problem of flat quantization loss surfaces through a lens of dynamically
changing bitwidth. Unlike existing techniques which either change the bit-precision abruptly or keep it fixed,
we propose to gradually alter the bitwidth as the model trains. To this end, we propose RelaxQAT, a continuous
relaxation of the QAT process. We also provide simple theoretical and empirical insights behind why RelaxQAT
helps the QAT performance. Our extensive experiments demonstrate up to 6.3% average accuracy gains on vision
and up to 7.7% better average accuracy on natural language tasks, compared to the leading QAT methods.

INTRODUCTION
Quantization has become the de facto strategy for deploy-
ing Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language
models (VLMs) on resource-constrained systems such as
phones and laptops (Nagel et al., 2021; Gong et al., 2025).
To address the substantial memory footprint, power con-
sumption, and cycle cost of inference of generative models,
quantization reduces the precision of weights and activa-
tions from a higher bitwidth to a significantly lower bitwidth.
This reduction in precision introduces quantization errors
which result in noisy model outputs and an associated accu-
racy degradation in the real-world applications (Song et al.,
2026). The prevailing paradigm that current hardware sup-
ports is quantization bitwidths in the range of 2 to 4 bits.
The lower the bits, the worse the quantization error, albeit
with a significantly better energy-efficiency. Although many
advanced techniques exist that address 4-bit precision (Lin
et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025a; Sun et al., 2020; Frantar et al.,
2022; Ashkboos et al., 2024), even lower bitwidth, such as
2-bit quantization, remains a critical challenge (Liu et al.,
2025b; Li et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2025). Hence, investigat-
ing newer quantization techniques to address 2-bit precision
for weights is of paramount importance.
For such low precisions, training-free methods known as
Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) have not been effective
at recovering the quantization error (Liu et al., 2025b; Yi

et al., 2026). Consequently, Quantization-Aware Training
(QAT) techniques have emerged as the standard methods to
convert a pretrained floating point model to a 2-bit weight
model (Song et al., 2026). Various QAT methods target
different challenges: LSQ (Esser et al., 2019) proposes a
learned scale as well as improvements to the gradient com-
putation to improve the QAT convergence. ParetoQ (Liu
et al., 2025b) proposes a new symmetric integer grid for
1.58- and 2-bit models. WinQ (Li et al., 2026) addresses
the presence of saddle points or flat regions in the quan-
tized loss surface. Progressive quantization methods such
as UPQ (Lee et al., 2025) focus on converting a model from
a floating point precision to 2-bits in multiple stages, e.g.,
going from floating point model to 4-bits first, and then
eventually quantizing the model to 2-bits.
Most QAT techniques in the literature result in an abrupt
change in bitwidth, either from floating point directly to 2-
bits (Liu et al., 2024b; Chen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b; Li
et al., 2026; Yi et al., 2026), or as in the case of UPQ (Lee
et al., 2025), from floating point to 4-bits first and then
suddenly to 2-bits. Once the desired bitwidth is reached,
the model is trained with no further changes in precision.
It is known that low-bit quantization loss surfaces usually
consist of highly flat surfaces (Li et al., 2026) due to the
nature of quantization itself. Hence, when we optimize on
a fixed bitwidth, the optimization may get stuck in those
flat portions of the loss surface. Indeed, increasing the bit-
precision of the weights can make the loss surface less flat
and can make it easier for the optimization to escape saddle
points. To this end, we hypothesize whether a dynamic
bitwidth during the QAT can be a solution to this problem.

A Continuous Relaxation of Quantization-Aware Training
Importantly, since abrupt changes in bitwidth can destabilize
the training, we need a solution that makes gradual changes
to the bitwidth as the training progresses.
Therefore, we ask the following key questions in this pa-
per: During QAT, can we dynamically relax the bitwidth of
a model in a continuous fashion? That is, instead of dis-
crete (abrupt) integer changes in the number of bits, can we
change the bitwidth in a continuous (floating point) domain?
If yes, how do we implement a floating point bitwidth and
what does it even mean?
To answer the above questions, we propose RelaxQAT, a
continuous relaxation of the QAT problem. The key idea be-
hind RelaxQAT is to create a schedule for the bitwidth and
change the underlying QAT equations, so that the extreme
values in the model weight distributions become floating
points which slowly turn into integers during the training.
Importantly, the dynamically changing bitwidth also dynam-
ically changes the number of quantization bins, which helps
the model escape saddle points and reach a better training
loss. With extensive experiments, we demonstrate that our
method achieves significantly better benchmark accuracies
on a variety of vision-language and natural language tasks.
Of note, our work focuses only on channelwise quantization
since it remains one of the most hardware-efficient quanti-
zation schemes, and is highly relevant to deploying large
generative models on constrained hardware such as Neural
Processing Units (NPUs).
Overall, we make the following key contributions:
1. We propose RelaxQAT, a continuous relaxation of the
QAT. To our knowledge, we are the first to dynamically
vary the bitwidth in a floating point domain and the
resulting number of quantization bins during the QAT.
2. We provide simple theoretical and empirical insights
behind why RelaxQAT helps the QAT performance.
3. For the challenging case of 2-bit weight only quantiza-
tion, we demonstrate that RelaxQAT achieves the state-
of-the-art accuracies on a variety of vision-language
and natural language benchmarks. We show an im-
provement in average accuracy of up to 6.3% on vision
benchmarks compared to the leading QAT baselines.
On natural language tasks, we show up to 7.7% better
average accuracy. In total, we conduct experiments
on five models of various sizes (0.6B to 4B), spanning
two VLMs, three LLMs, and multiple model (Gemma
and Qwen) families. We consider five leading QAT
baselines and each model is trained on 10B tokens.
4. Finally, we also show a proof-of-concept that Re-
laxQAT can be used for other bitwidths such as ternary
(1.58-bit) quantization. We show up to 4.2% accuracy
gains for this quantization scheme.

RELATED WORK
We review briefly important quantization methods from the
literature to set the stage for our proposed RelaxQAT al-
gorithm. One of the early work in low-bit quantization
was the Learned Step Quantization, introduced by (Esser
et al., 2019). LSQ’s key innovation was to treat the quanti-
zation step size itself as a learnable parameter .(Choi et al.,
2018), proposed learning activation clipping thresholds dur-
ing training while (Bhalgat et al., 2020) proposed learning
both scale and offset.
Building on LSQ, (Liu et al., 2025b) proposed ParetoQ
which introduced Stretched Elastic Quantizer (SEQ) for
ternary and 2-bit quantization. It is shown that although
LSQ performed well in higher-precision (e.g., 4-bit quantiza-
tion), its effectiveness degrades significantly in the extreme
low-bit regimes. To address this, SEQ employs a more sym-
metric quantization grid, leading to improved performance.
Inference scaling laws for models w.r.t quantization have
been extensively studied in (Dettmers & Zettlemoyer, 2023),
(ˇSubi´c & Cozzini, 2026), and (Liu et al., 2025b).
BitNet was proposed in (Hugging Face, 2024), which in-
troduced the idea of gradually transitioning model weights
towards the target low-bit representation through a prede-
fined schedule (e.g., linear schedule). This is accomplished
by linearly combining the latent weights with appropriately
scaled version (based on a schedule) of difference of FP
latent weight and the desired quantized weight. However, in
our proposed RelaxQAT, the bitwidth itself changes based
on a schedule. Other follow up works in Bitnet w.r.t activa-
tion quantization, 1-bit optimzation and efficient inference
are detailed in (Wang et al., 2024), (Wang et al., 2025c),
(Wang et al., 2025d).
(Li et al., 2026) investigates the root cause of slow conver-
gence observed in quantization-aware training (QAT) for
extreme low bit quantization. It is shown that low-bit train-
ing tends to become trapped in flat regions around saddle
points of the loss landscape, resulting in slower optimization.
To mitigate this issue, an optimization strategy is proposed
that periodically interpolates the quantized weights with the
latent weights to escape the saddle point. Further, (Xu et al.,
2025), (Xu et al., 2026),(Wei et al., 2022), (Lv et al., 2026),
(Shao et al., 2024), (Lee et al., 2024), (Wang et al., 2025b),
(Wang et al., 2025a) analyse several low-bit training meth-
ods and how outlier-aware quantization schedules improve
convergence and accuracy at extreme bit widths.
A direction closely related to our proposal is the progressive
quantization framework introduced in Unifying Block-wise
PTQ and Distillation-based QAT for Progressive Quantiza-
tion (UPQ) (Lee et al., 2025). UPQ proposes a two-stage
quantization pipeline in which a full-precision model is con-
verted to an intermediate representation (e.g., INT4) using

block-wise post-training quantization (PTQ), followed by
INT2 QAT initialized from the intermediate checkpoint. In
contrast, the proposed RelaxQAT generalizes the notion
of progressive quantization by replacing the fixed transi-
tion (FP to INT4 to INT2) with a continuously evolving bit
schedule. We hypothesize that this continuous transition
enables a smoother optimization trajectory as shown next.

PROPOSED RELAXQAT
RelaxQAT consists of two parts: (i) set bitwidth using a
smooth schedule during training, and (ii) modify existing
quantization equations such as Stretched Elastic Quantizer
(SEQ) (Liu et al., 2025b) to make sure the extreme values of
the weight distribution become floating points so they can
smoothly vary as the bitwidth changes. We describe these
components below.

3.2
RelaxSEQ
QAT first replaces each model weight with its corresponding
quantized-dequantized (QDQ) weight which is computed
as a function of a latent floating point weight θ and its
channelwise scale α. Specifically, given a θ ∈Rn×m, the
latent floating point weight with n output channels and m
input channels and a channelwise scale α ∈Rn, various
quantization schemes compute the QDQ weights in different
ways (Esser et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2025b). Then, the model
is trained with the latent weight θ and the scale α as the
trainable parameters, where the QDQ weight is computed in
every forward pass and passed on to the model layers. The
associated backward pass is also modified using Straight-
Through Estimators (STE), etc. (Esser et al., 2019).

EXPERIMENTS
5.1
Experimental Setup
We perform experiments on Qwen3 (0.6B/4B), Qwen3.5
(2B/4B) and Gemma3-1B models in the language domain
and Qwen3.5 (2B/4B) models in the vision-language do-
main. We compare our method against five state-of-the-
art QAT baselines, including ParetoQ (Liu et al., 2025b),
LLM-QAT (Liu et al., 2024b), UPQ (Lee et al., 2025), Bit-
Net (Hugging Face, 2024), and WinQ (Li et al., 2026). Ad-
ditionally, we evaluate AdaScale (AIMET, 2026), a PTQ
method for comparison. For BitNet, since (Hugging Face,
2024) is only for 1.58 bits and has no learnable scale, we
adopt ParetoQ’s SEQ function to get the 2-bit QDQ weight
and use it inside the BitNet algorithm.
The training data consists of a mixture of DCLM-Edu (Allal
et al., 2025), tulu-3-sft-olmo-2-mixture (Allen Institute for
AI) and xlam-function-calling-60k (Liu et al., 2024a). For
vision-language model training, we additionally include
Llama-Nemotron-VLM-Dataset-v1 (NVIDIA). We train all
models on 10B tokens. We use the same initial learning rate
of 0.0001 for all models and baselines with a cosine learning
rate schedule that decays to nearly zero. The smaller models
are trained on a single node of eight H100 GPUs whereas
the bigger models are trained on two A100 nodes. Note that,
we only do weight only quantization in all experiments.
We select n = 1 full cycle (after an initial half cycle) for all
experiments. For maximum schedule steps ms, we perform
a grid search between {400, 800, 1200}. We also conduct
a detailed ablation study to show that RelaxQAT is robust
across hyperparameter choices for n and ms. Also, we set
bs = 4 and be = 2 bits, signifying a 4-bit to 2-bit schedule.
In the language domain, we evaluate RelaxQAT on five
zero-shot common sense reasoning tasks. These include
Arc-easy, Arc-challenge (Clark et al., 2018), HellaSwag
(Zellers et al., 2019), Winogrande (Sakaguchi et al., 2021),
and PIQA (Bisk et al., 2020). We also report five-shot
MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2020) (loglikelihood task) and
IFEval (Zhou et al., 2023) (generative task) benchmarks,
each focusing on evaluating the knowledge and instruction
following capabilities of the model. In vision domain, we
evaluate on MMMU (Yue et al., 2024), DocVQA (Mathew
et al., 2021), and ScienceQA (Lu et al., 2022) benchmarks.

achieves a nearly 6% higher IFEval score than the second
best method.
5.5
Does RelaxQAT work for Ternary Quantization?
Finally, we investigate whether RelaxQAT works for other
quantization schemes such as ternary (1.58) bits: {−1, 0, 1}
integer grid. Table 12 in Appendix F shows the accuracy
of ternary Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 models. As ev-
ident, RelaxQAT performs the best with 4.2% higher accu-
racy than ParetoQ. Similar results are observed for Table 13
in Appendix F for the ternary Qwen3.5-2B model. Re-
laxQAT outperforms ParetoQ by 1.38%. Hence, RelaxQAT
is a versatile technique that strongly outperforms leading
methods for both ternary (1.58-bit) and 2-bit quantization.
6
CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In this paper, we have explored a new paradigm which aims
to create a continuous relaxation of the QAT process. To this
end, we have proposed RelaxQAT which dynamically varies
the bitwidth during training in a continuous floating point do-
main. RelaxQAT gradually alters the bit precision of model
weights in order to accelerate the early training convergence,
as well as to escape any flat regions in the low-bit quantiza-
tion loss surfaces. We have also presented simple theoretical
and empirical insights into why RelaxQAT helps QAT. Our
extensive experiments demonstrate accuracy gains of up to
6.3% on vision and up to 7.7% on natural language tasks,
compared to the leading QAT methods.
Finally, although we have considered weight only quantiza-
tion in this work, RelaxQAT can also be applied to activation
or KV-cache quantization. We leave this for future work.

MORE DETAILS ON EXPERIMENTS
We use a total batch size of 256 for Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-

2B and Qwen3.5-4B models. We implement the AdamW

For evaluation, we select a variety of tasks.
For in-
stance, common sense reasoning (CSR) benchmarks in-
clude five tasks: Arc-easy, Arc-Challenge, HellaSwag,
PIQA and WinoGrande which focus on everyday knowl-

We also evaluate on benchmarks such as MMLU and IFEval.
MMLU evaluates the model on factual recall and basic rea-
soning across 57 subjects whereas IFEval evaluates whether
the model is following natural language instructions. While
the CSR and MMLU tasks are multiple-choice questions,
comparing the log-likelihood of each answer, IFEval is a
generation task. We report accuracy norm metric for Arc-
Easy, Arc-Challenge, Hellaswag, PIQA and accuracy for
WingoGrande. IFEval reports four metrics namely prompt-
level-strict-acc, inst-level-strict-acc, prompt-level-loose-acc,
inst-level-loose-acc. For our experiments, we take an aver-
age of all four metrics.
