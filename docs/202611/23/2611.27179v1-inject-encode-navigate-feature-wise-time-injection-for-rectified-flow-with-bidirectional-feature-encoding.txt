Inject, Encode, Navigate: Feature-Wise Time Injection for Rectified Flow
with Bidirectional Feature Encoding
Xiaogang Wang1 Debasish Mishra1 Jia Hao Toh2 Wangsheng Liu2 Yash Shah1
Abstract— Vision-model-based robotic navigation has gained
increasing popularity, yet existing approaches still struggle
to deliver effective and efficient navigation performance, e.g.,
diffusion-based policies that require costly iterative denoising.
In this work, we propose three improvements to bridge these
gaps. First, we adopt a rectified flow framework to reduce the
required inference steps to one while achieving comparable or
even superior performance. Second, we leverage a bidirectional
Mamba-based visual encoder that employs selective state-space
modeling to more effectively capture bidirectional temporal
dependencies across observation frames, even with longer input
sequences. Most importantly, we find that na¨ıvely combining
these two components to balance accuracy with inference speed
yields no further performance gains or even produces inferior
results. Upon extensive analysis, we realized that the root
cause lies in the fact that the Hydra-derived temporal context
is shared unchanged across all flow timesteps, and timestep
information is introduced only within the velocity network.
This weak coupling limits the model’s ability to adaptively
exploit temporal visual features at different flow states. To
address this, we propose a feature-wise timestep conditioning
design that explicitly conditions the visual embedding on the
flow timestep, enabling noise-level-aware trajectory generation,
thereby restoring a meaningful gradient pathway and enabling
coherent interaction between the rectified flow and the temporal
encoder. Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments
across multiple datasets demonstrate that our approach consis-
tently outperforms both baseline and competitive methods.
I. INTRODUCTION
Autonomous visual navigation is the ability of a robot
to perceive its surroundings through onboard sensors and
navigate toward a designated goal without relying on pre-
built maps, which is a cornerstone capability for deploying
intelligent agents in the real world. The importance of this
task spans a broad spectrum of applications. In security and
surveillance, mobile robots need to patrol large facilities,
detect anomalies, and autonomously navigate to points of
interest in dynamic and unstructured environments. In search
and rescue, agents are required to traverse disaster-stricken
terrains where GPS signals are unavailable and pre-existing
maps are rendered obsolete. Moreover, warehouse logis-
tics, last-mile delivery, agricultural inspection, and assistive
robotics for the elderly all demand robust, real-time visual
navigation that generalizes across diverse and previously
unseen environments.

navigation agents that can operate reliably outside controlled
laboratory settings. Early learning-based approaches such as
ViNT [1] introduced the idea of training a general-purpose
navigation policy across diverse robotic platforms and envi-
ronments, demonstrating impressive cross-embodiment trans-

by incorporating a diffusion-based action generation pol-
icy, leveraging the expressiveness of Denoising Diffusion
Probabilistic Models (DDPM) [7] to produce multi-modal
waypoint distributions and achieving strong performance
in topological navigation tasks. However, diffusion-based
policies inherently require multiple iterative denoising steps
at inference time which introduces additional computa-
tional latency. Furthermore, existing methods mostly rely on
transformer-based [8] or convolutional encoders to process
multi-frame visual observations. While effective for short
observation histories, these encoders struggle to capture long-
range temporal dependencies as the number of input frames
grows, due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention [8]
or the limited receptive fields of convolutional architectures.
This results in less accurate waypoint predictions, especially
in scenarios that require reasoning over extended navigation
histories to resolve ambiguities in the environment.
To address these challenges, we propose three synergis-
tic improvements built upon state-of-the-art diffusion-based
framework NoMad [2]. Firstly, to speed up the inference
steps, we propose to replace the conventional DDPM de-
noising process [7] with a Rectified Flow Matching [9], [10]
formulation that reformulates waypoint prediction as learn-
ing straight-line trajectories in data space. This principled
reformulation reduces the required inference steps from ten
to just one via a single Euler integration step, while achiev-

ing on-par or even superior waypoint prediction accuracy
compared to the DDPM strategy [7]. Secondly, to strengthen
temporal context modeling across multi-frame observations,
we propose to adopt the bidirectional Mamba-based model
Hydra [11] to process the input observation images inspired
by [3]. Unlike the transformer’s quadratic self-attention,
Mamba’s linear recurrence mechanism captures long-range
temporal dependencies far more effectively as the context
window grows, yielding richer goal-conditioned visual rep-
resentations particularly when processing large numbers of
input frames. In our experiments, we are able to consume
up to ten frames. Thirdly and most critically, we observe
that naively combining the above two improvements does not
provide cumulative gains and can even degrade performance.
We attribute this behavior to insufficient interaction between
the flow state and the temporally enriched visual represen-
tation. Specifically, the Hydra encoder produces a context
vector that remains unchanged across flow timesteps, while
the timestep is provided only to the downstream velocity
network. As a result, the same visual feature representation
is used throughout flow-matching training, regardless of the
current interpolation state. To address this, we introduce a
lightweight feature-wise conditioning mechanism that injects
the flow timestep into the visual conditioning signal before it
enters the denoising network, restoring noise-level awareness
so that the two components work in synergy. Extensive
quantitative and qualitative experiments across multiple robot
navigation benchmarks demonstrate that our unified frame-
work achieves superior waypoint prediction accuracy and
navigation success rates compared to existing works.
Last but not least, we further extend our proposed ar-
chitecture with a detection-guided mechanism to tackle a
two-phase security inspection task using the Deep Robotics
X30 Pro quadruped robot: first navigating the robot toward
a target object, then executing a circular motion around
it for detailed inspection. By incorporating a lightweight
object detection head [12] into our framework, the robot can
approach a specific target without relying on a goal frame
image, using the bounding box area as an additional indicator
for autonomous stopping. This mechanism builds naturally
upon our flow matching policy and the learned model gen-
erates the overall navigation and circling trajectories, while
the detection head enhances target-aware approaching. An
illustration of our navigation task is shown in Figure 1.
Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:
• We carefully incorporate rectified flow matching into
the diffusion-based framework [2] to reduce inference
steps from teens to one while also improving waypoint
prediction accuracy.
• We propose to replace the transformer-based tempo-
ral encoder with a bidirectional Mamba state-space
model Hydra, which more effectively captures long-
range temporal dependencies across multi-frame obser-
vations with linear computational complexity.
• We introduce a feature-wise timestep conditioning
mechanism that directly modulates the temporally en-
riched visual context according to the current flow
state, enabling stronger interaction between temporal
perception and trajectory generation.
• We conduct extensive experiments on multiple datasets,
demonstrating that our unified framework achieves
state-of-the-art performances.
• We extend our framework with a detection-guided
mechanism for a two-phase security inspection task,
where the object detector enables goal-image-free target
approaching.
II. RELATED WORKS
A. Vision-Based Robot Navigation
Vision-based navigation has evolved from classical map-
based planning to modern learning-based methods. Re-
cent works have focused on general-purpose, transferable
navigation policies including ViNT [1] learned a univer-
sal policy from heterogeneous multi-robot datasets with
zero-shot transfer, GNM [13] established the foundation
model paradigm for goal-conditioned navigation, and No-
MaD [2] integrated diffusion-based action generation to
capture multi-modal action distributions for state-of-the-art
topological navigation. Despite this progress, these methods
still face limitations in larger temporal context modeling, and
diffusion-based method needs several inference steps to get
reasonable results.
B. Diffusion Models for Robot Control
Diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in
image generation and have been increasingly adopted for
robot control. Diffusion Policy [14] pioneered applying
DDPM [7] to visuomotor policy learning, demonstrating that
iterative denoising effectively captures multi-modal action
distributions in manipulation tasks. This approach has since
been extended to navigation [2] and other robotic domains.
However, diffusion-based policies suffer from high inference
latency due to multiple sequential denoising steps. While
DDIM [15] has attempted to reduce sampling steps through
non-Markovian processes, our work takes a fundamentally
different approach by reformulating action generation using
flow matching [9], which inherently supports single-step
inference without requiring distillation or additional training
stages.
C. Flow Matching and Rectified Flow
Flow matching [10], [9] has emerged as an elegant alter-
native to diffusion models, learning a deterministic velocity
field that transports samples from a simple prior to the
data distribution, rather than reversing a stochastic noising
process. Rectified Flow [9] further simplifies this by defining
straight-line interpolation paths, enabling accurate single-
step Euler integration. While flow matching has shown
competitive or superior performance in image [16] and
video generation with significant sampling efficiency gains,
its application in robotics remains underexplored. To our
knowledge, no prior work has systematically investigated
flow matching for visual navigation or its integration with
advanced temporal encoders, which is the focus of our work.

III. METHOD
In this section, we present our proposed framework for
efficient and accurate visual navigation. Our baseline archi-
tecture is built upon the NoMaD [2] model, and we introduce
three principled improvements that collectively address the
key limitations of existing navigation policies. Our frame-
work consists of: (1) a rectified flow matching formulation
that replaces the conventional DDPM denoising [15] process
to enable single-step inference; (2) a bidirectional Hydra [19]
encoder that replaces the transformer-based temporal encoder
to more effectively capture long-range dependencies across
multi-frame observations; and (3) a feature-wise conditioning
mechanism that resolves the critical incompatibility between
the Hydra encoder and flow matching, enabling the two
improvements to work synergistically. An overview of the
complete architecture is illustrated in Figure 2.

The NoMaD [2] baseline employs a DDPM-based [7] dif-
fusion policy to generate waypoint action trajectories. During
training, the forward diffusion process progressively corrupts
a ground-truth action trajectory a0 by adding Gaussian noise

with rectified flow matching [9], which enables high-quality
action trajectory generation in as few as a single integration
step.

(3) the continuous-time formulation provides a natural frame-
work for trading off between speed and quality by adjusting
the number of Euler steps if needed.

observation history becomes long.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate our pro-
posed framework across multiple public benchmarks and
a challenging internal dataset. We compare against state-
of-the-art models ViNT [1] and NoMaD [2] together with
several ablation variants to validate the contribution of
each proposed component. In addition to offline quantitative
evaluation, we deploy our model on a physical quadruped
robot to assess real-world navigation performance. In our
experiments, F10 represents for increasing NoMad [2]’s input
number of image frames from three to ten, which is a ablation
study to verify the effectiveness of our architecture when
inputting long image sequences; Ours (RF) represents for
our model with rectified flow, Ours (Hydra) represents for
our model with Hydra encoder, Ours (Na¨ıve) represents for
the na¨ıve combination of RF and Hydra, and Ours (Full)
represens for our full architecture.
A. Datasets
We evaluate our framework on two widely used public
benchmarks and one internally collected dataset, covering
diverse environments and navigation scenarios. For fair com-
parison, we train separate models for each dataset across
all our model variants and baseline methods from existing
works.
RECON [22]. The RECON dataset contains navigation tra-
jectories collected across a variety of outdoor environments
including parks, trails, and urban sidewalks. The dataset
features diverse visual conditions such as varying lighting,
seasonal changes, and different terrain types. We select 150
representative trajectories for train and 20 trajectories for
testing.
TartanDrive [23]. The TartanDrive dataset consists of off-
road driving data collected on an all-terrain vehicle equipped
with multiple sensors. The dataset features challenging un-
structured environments with rough terrain, vegetation, and
varying weather conditions. This dataset tests the model’s
ability to handle visually complex and geometrically chal-
lenging navigation scenarios. We select 150 representative
trajectories for train and 20 trajectories for testing.
Internal Quadruped Dataset. To evaluate our framework in
a more challenging real-world setting, we collect a custom
dataset using the Deep Robotics X30 Pro quadruped plat-
form. The robot is equipped with a front-facing RGB camera
and records navigation trajectories across indoor office en-
vironments, outdoor paths, and semi-structured warehouse
settings. Critically, our internal task is more demanding
than standard point-goal navigation: the robot must not only
navigate to the target location but also execute a circling
motion around the target upon arrival. This compound task
requires the policy to generate smooth, continuous trajecto-
ries that testing the model’s ability to produce diverse and
temporally coherent action sequences. The dataset contains
120 navigation trajectories, split into 80%/10%/10% for
training, validation, and testing, respectively.
B. Implementation Details
All models are trained using the AdamW optimizer with
an initial learning rate of 1 × 10−4, weight decay of 1 ×
10−2, and a cosine annealing learning rate schedule with a
linear warmup of 500 steps. The batch size is set to 256
across all experiments. For the flow matching formulation,
the timestep t is uniformly sampled from U (0,1) during
training. For real-world deployment on the X30 Pro platform,
the model runs on an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin module.
For experimental comparisons, we fine-tune ViNT [1] and
NoMad [2] using suggested hyper-parameters of their official
code.

Inference Time. We report the average time to generate
one action trajectory, measured on an NVIDIA RTX PRO
6000 GPU in Table V. Inference latency is dominated by the
number of sequential passes through the action (denoising)
network rather than by the visual encoder, since the encoder
runs only once per prediction whereas the denoising network
is invoked repeatedly. NoMaD [2] requires ten sequential
U-Net passes to iteratively denoise the trajectory, while
our rectified-flow formulation recovers the trajectory in a
single Euler integration step. Adopting the bidirectional
Hydra encoder does add per-call cost, which is shown in
Ours (Hydra) at 98.7 ms under the same ten-step sampling,
but this cost is incurred only once, and Mamba’s linear-time
recurrence keeps its growth modest even as the number of
input frames increases. Consequently, combining single-step
rectified flow with the Hydra encoder (Ours (Full)) reduces
the latency to 68.4 ms, which is not only below the multi-
step Hydra variant but also faster than the NoMaD baseline’s
79.9 ms [2], despite our model processing substantially
longer observation sequences.

F. Real-World Deployment with Navigation and Circling
To evaluate the practical effectiveness of our framework
beyond offline metrics, we deploy all methods on a physical
robot platform tasked with navigating toward a designated
target and subsequently performing a circular inspection
motion around it. We train two separate models for the
two phases, namely navigation toward the target and circular
inspection, and combine them during deployment. Both mod-
els share the same architecture but are trained on different
data corresponding to their respective phases. We report two
metrics: navigation success rate (SR) and full detection time
(FDT), defined as the percentage of the circular inspection
phase during which the robot maintains continuous full
visibility of the target. Results are summarized in Table VI.
We designed a comprehensive set of experiments across
five distinct starting points, each tested three times for a total
of fifteen trials. The robot’s camera is equipped with a 130-
degree field of view (FOV), which informed the selection of
starting positions. 1) The first starting point was positioned
directly in front of the target object at 5 meters, serving
as a baseline for straight-line navigation accuracy. 2) The
second and third starting points were placed at the leftmost
and rightmost extremes of the camera’s FOV at the 5-meter
range, which is designed to test whether the robot could


accurately reach the object from the farthest perceivable
positions, thereby verifying that cumulative navigation error
remained sufficiently small over long distances. 3) The fourth
and fifth starting points were positioned at a very short
vertical distance of 1.5 meters from the robot, but at the
maximum horizontal offset where the camera could just
barely detect the object at the edges of its FOV. These near-
but-lateral positions were specifically chosen to test whether
the robot could execute a sufficiently large turning angle
within a very short distance to orient itself toward and reach
the target. The examples are shown in Figure 5.
The quantitative comparisons among different models are
shown in Table VI. Existing works or our model variants suf-
fer from the inability to simultaneously achieve a sufficiently
large turning angle in a short period of time and maintain
sufficiently small accumulated error over long distances,
and they failed 2 times out of 15 trials. The performance
gap in SR (86.7% →100%) demonstrates that our model
provides a meaningful improvement in navigation reliability,
particularly in challenging starting positions. Most notably,
Ours-Full achieves the highest FDT of 80.2% among all
methods. This metric is particularly meaningful as it directly
reflects the quality of the predicted circular trajectory during
the inspection phase: a higher FDT indicates that the robot
maintains a consistent and appropriate radius around the
target, keeping it fully within the sensor’s field of view
throughout the circular motion. This capability is essential
for security inspection scenarios, where the robot must per-
form a complete circumnavigation of a potentially hazardous
target to gather comprehensive visual information from all
angles. We attached a video showing the robot navigating
toward the bag from both lateral directions and executing a
full circular motion around the target.
V. CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we presented a unified framework for
efficient vision-based robotic navigation comprising three
contributions: a rectified flow formulation that reduces multi-
step denoising to a single inference step, a bidirectional
Mamba-based visual encoder for capturing temporal de-
pendencies across observation frames, and a feature-wise
timestep conditioning mechanism that bridges the two by
modulating encoded features via learned scale and shift
parameters derived from the flow timestep. Our key finding
is that na¨ıvely combining rectified flow with the temporal
encoder degrades performance, and our feature-wise con-
ditioning addresses this limitation by allowing the visual
context to adapt as a function of the flow timestep during
training. Experiments across multiple datasets and real-world
deployment demonstrate consistent improvements over the
corresponding baselines.
