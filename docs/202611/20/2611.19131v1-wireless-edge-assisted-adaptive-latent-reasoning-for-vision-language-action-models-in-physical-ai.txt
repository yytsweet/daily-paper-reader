Wireless Edge-Assisted Adaptive Latent Reasoning
for Vision-Language-Action Models in Physical AI
Fatih Temiz and Melike Erol-Kantarci, Fellow, IEEE
School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, University of Ottawa, Ottawa, Canada
Emails: {ftemi033, melike.erolkantarci}@uottawa.ca
Abstract—Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable em-
bodied artificial intelligence (AI) by translating visual observa-
tions and language instructions into robotic actions. Emerging
use cases in physical AI are expected to drive the need for
efficient inference and communication, as the computational
demands of the VLA models pose challenges for real-time control
with limited onboard resources. Test-time scaling through latent
reasoning allows these models to refine hidden representations
without generating explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) tokens, but
introduces a trade-off between task success, inference latency,
and energy consumption. In this paper, we address the efficient
collaborative inference under a realistic communication scenario.
We propose a wireless edge-assisted framework for adaptive
latent reasoning (WEALR) that augments onboard computation
with edge computing. A decision-making agent jointly selects the
execution location and latent-reasoning convergence threshold.
We formulate this joint adaptation as a constrained Markov
decision process (CMDP). A Lagrangian-constrained deep re-
inforcement learning (CDRL) approach, namely trust region
policy optimization Lagrangian (TRPO-Lag), learns to balance
task performance and resource consumption under varying
wireless conditions and task requirements. We evaluate the
framework using LIBERO manipulation tasks, profiled VLA
inference costs, and ray-traced wireless channels. Experimental
results demonstrate that TRPO-Lag achieves up to 23% higher
feasibility-weighted task success rate compared to benchmark
CDRL methods.

I. INTRODUCTION
Physical artificial intelligence (AI) brings perception, rea-
soning and action into embodied systems that interact with
the physical world [1]. Vision-language-action (VLA) models,
such as OpenVLA [2], support this capability by translating
visual observations and language instructions into robotic
actions. Advances in these models create opportunities for
generalizable physical AI, but their computational demands
challenge deployment on resource-constrained robots. Task
performance must therefore be balanced against onboard com-
putation, energy consumption, and latency requirements [3],
[4]. In particular, timely robotic control requires commu-
nication and inference to jointly satisfy a latency service-
level objective (SLO). We refer to robots capable of visual
perception, instruction understanding, planning, reasoning, and
action execution as physical AI devices (PADs) in this paper.
Edge computing augments limited onboard computation by
enabling PADs to offload inference to nearby edge servers,
but its benefits depend on wireless propagation conditions
[5]. This deployment perspective aligns with the vision of
using radio access network (RAN) infrastructure to support
AI applications at the network edge, such as mobile switching
offices (MSO) [6]. However, faster edge computation comes
at the cost of wireless transmission delay. In indoor envi-
ronments such as homes and warehouses, blockage and mul-
tipath propagation create heterogeneous line-of-sight (LOS)
and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) conditions. Changes in PAD
position affect achievable uplink (UL) rates and can offset the
computational advantage of edge execution. Effective offload-
ing must therefore account for wireless conditions alongside
computation and control-latency requirements. Evaluating this
tradeoff requires environment-specific channel models that
capture blockage, multipath propagation, and spatial variations
in link quality, which ray tracing can provide [7], [8].
Reliable physical interaction requires VLA models to inter-
pret diverse visual observations and language instructions and
generate appropriate actions across tasks of varying difficulty.
Supporting these capabilities motivates increasing model ca-
pacity through parameter scaling or allocating additional com-
putation during inference.Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE)
architectures enable parameter scaling while limiting computa-
tion per input by activating only a subset of model parameters
[9]. However, their memory and computational requirements
can still challenge deployment on resource-constrained PADs.
Test-time scaling offers a complementary approach that adjusts
computational effort within a fixed model, making it particu-
larly attractive for wireless edge deployment, where available
resources and latency requirements limit inference costs [5].
Unlike explicit reasoning methods such as chain-of-thought
(CoT), which generate intermediate text through sequential
decoding, latent reasoning refines hidden representations with-
out producing intermediate text tokens, potentially reducing
the decoding overhead and latency associated with textual
reasoning. The recurrent-depth architecture in [10] enables
large language models (LLMs), which serve as backbones
of VLAs, to vary computational depth through shared pa-
rameters, offering adjustable inference effort within a fixed
model footprint. For robotic inference, recurrent-depth VLA
(RD-VLA) applies a related principle through a weight-tied
recurrent action head and a convergence-based stopping cri-
terion to adapt the number of reasoning iterations across
observations [11]. For example, grasping a small object may
require more reasoning than moving through an unobstructed
region. A single convergence threshold can therefore yield

different reasoning depths across stages of a task and different
tasks. Additional reasoning may improve action quality but
also increases inference latency and resource consumption,
motivating adaptive control of reasoning effort. These con-
siderations motivate jointly selecting VLA execution location
and reasoning effort under wireless, energy, and computation
constraints.
Related studies address complementary aspects of collab-
orative inference. The device-cloud framework in [12] uses
resource-constrained reinforcement learning (RL) to select
LLM inference locations and multimodal inputs. Also, the
EcoVLA approach proposed in [13] selects energy-efficient
VLA co-inference schemes under real-time constraints, adapt-
ing to network fluctuations and edge workload variations. The
authors of [14] propose MORES, a reasoning-as-a-service
framework that distributes recurrent hidden-state updates be-
tween devices and servers, jointly adapting reasoning com-
putation and latent-feature pruning under heterogeneous wire-
less conditions. While conversational LLM services typically
prioritize response quality, VLAs must deliver actions within
deadlines imposed by ongoing physical control [3].
Meeting these deadlines requires balancing task success
against communication delay and resource consumption. Cu-
mulative budgets couple decisions across requests, and shared
edge resources couple decisions across PADs, motivating a
constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) formulation.
Constrained deep reinforcement learning (CDRL) addresses
such problems by separating performance objectives from
explicit operating limits [15]. This motivates the use of CDRL
methods, including Lagrangian variants of proximal policy op-
timization (PPO) and trust region policy optimization (TRPO),
as well as constrained policy optimization (CPO), for resource
management in wireless networks. In particular, PPO-Lag has
been adopted for joint unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) altitude
control and Internet of Things (IoT) channel access [16].
In this paper, we propose Wireless Edge-Assisted Adaptive
Latent Reasoning (WEALR), a framework that jointly adapts
VLA execution location and reasoning effort. For each robotic
task request, an agent selects local or edge execution and
a reasoning convergence threshold. This threshold governs
termination of the latent reasoning loop while allowing the
realized reasoning depth to vary across observations within
the task. We use TRPO-Lag, which learns the policy and
constraint multipliers, connecting adaptive latent reasoning
with wireless offloading and cumulative resource management.
The key contributions are as follows:
1) We develop WEALR to jointly select VLA execution
location and reasoning threshold according to task re-
quirements, wireless conditions, and available resources.
2) We formulate a CMDP with per-PAD energy, shared
edge-reasoning, and SLO constraints, and apply TRPO-
Lag to optimize robotic task success.
3) We evaluate task success, feasibility, and policy behavior
using LIBERO manipulation tasks [17], profiled VLA
inference costs, and ray-traced channels under varying
wireless conditions and resource budgets.
II. SYSTEM MODEL AND PROBLEM
FORMULATION
We consider an edge-computing system comprising N
PADs and an edge server each equipped with AI acceleration
hardware co-located with an indoor base station (BS), as
shown in Fig. 1 (a). Both PADs and the server host pretrained
VLA models with preloaded checkpoints; cold-start latency
is neglected [13]. Each episode comprises K requests, with
request k originating from PAD ik and belonging to task suite
qk. The controller selects an execution location and reasoning
threshold for each request, fixed throughout the task. Requests
are served sequentially, with only one active UL transmission
at a time [14]. We use synchronous VLA inference: each cycle
begins after the previous action chunk is executed, avoiding
potentially stale observations in asynchronous execution [3].

Task-aware heuristic (TAH), which selects a fixed action per

B. Results

ing performance and normalized CMDP constraint costs. All
methods improve task success during training, reaching ap-
proximately 92-94 successful tasks per episode, equivalent
to the average episode reward. However, maximizing success
alone does not ensure feasible operation. PPO and TRPO
maintain SLO violation rates approximately four times the
prescribed limit, while their edge reasoning costs approach
or exceed the budget during training. In contrast, TRPO-Lag
and PPO-Lag reduce SLO violations below the limit while
maintaining comparable task success. TRPO-Lag stabilizes at
approximately 0.8 of the maximum PAD energy budget and
0.7 of the edge reasoning budget. CPO also controls resource
consumption, but its SLO violation rate remains close to the

V. CONCLUSION
In conclusion, this paper proposed the WEALR framework
for adaptive latent reasoning of VLA models in wireless edge-
assisted robotic networks. By formulating the joint optimiza-
tion of execution location and latent convergence threshold
as a CMDP, our Lagrangian-CDRL approach dynamically
balances task success against PAD energy budgets, shared
edge-reasoning limits, and latency SLO constraints under
time-varying wireless conditions. Evaluations across LIBERO
manipulation tasks with profiled hardware costs and realistic
channel models demonstrate that the proposed framework
effectively satisfies resource constraints while achieving up
to a 23% gain in feasibility-weighted task success rate over
baselines. Future work may explore concurrent robotic task
requests with joint wireless and reasoning optimization.
