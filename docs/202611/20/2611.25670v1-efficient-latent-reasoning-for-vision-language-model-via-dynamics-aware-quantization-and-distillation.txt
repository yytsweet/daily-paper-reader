Abstract
Efficient
chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in vision-language mod-
els (VLMs) requires reducing both intermediate reasoning
steps and their execution cost. Latent CoT methods com-
press textual rationales into continuous thoughts, yet their
full-precision (FP) models retain substantial storage and
computation costs. Lowering numerical precision could re-
duce these remaining costs, yet direct quantization severely
degrades reasoning accuracy,
particularly with 4-bit
weights and activations. Operator and perturbation analy-
ses reveal key sensitivity and direction-dependent predictive
effects of continuous-thought errors, motivating preser-
vation of information retrieval and subsequent reasoning
beyond reconstruction accuracy.
We therefore introduce
QL-VLM, a dynamics-aware Quantization framework
for efficient Latent reasoning in Vision-Language Models
(VLMs).
First, Dynamics-Guided Quantization (DGQ)
uses
nonuniform
codebooks
that
preserve
rotary-key
structure, mitigating quantization-induced distortion in
vision-language information retrieval. Second, Dynamics-
Preserving Distillation (DPD) aligns continuous thoughts,
latent-query readouts, and predictions with a full-precision
teacher, maintaining predictive consistency through subse-
quent low-precision reasoning. On ScienceQA-IMG with
Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct, 4-bit QL-VLM achieves 90.43%
accuracy and improves baseline by 40.33%, with 16.64×
fewer bit-operations and 6.40× lower measured latency
than explicit CoT.

Multimodal
chain-of-thought
(CoT)

vision-

at an inference cost that grows along two axes. The first
is the number of decoding steps: on MMStar [1], explicit
CoT with Qwen3-VL-8B spends 7.24 s generating 142.1
reasoning tokens against 0.47 s encoding the input [10],
and thinking-style models further push output to several
thousand tokens per example (about 4.4K for Kimi-VL-

token or thought requires a forward pass through a multi-
billion-parameter backbone whose weights, activations,
and attention history are stored and read at full precision.
Efficient deployment must reduce both.
Prior work largely treats the two axes separately. Token
pruning [2] and concise-reasoning objectives [34] shorten
the sequence, and latent CoT [8, 10, 21] goes further by re-
placing textual rationales with a few continuous thoughts;
each remaining step, however, still executes a full-precision
model. On ScienceQA [15] with Qwen2-VL-2B (Fig. 1),
full-precision latent CoT [21] cuts generation from 56.28
tokens to six thoughts and six answer tokens, but the model
still occupies 4.89 GB and its theoretical per-answer com-
pute falls by only 8% (223.10 to 205.65 TBitOps). Low-bit
quantization addresses the second axis: post-training and
quantization-aware methods [6, 13, 31] reach 8-bit, and in-
creasingly 4-bit, weights and activations with modest loss.
Yet, the two remedies do not combine additively. Quan-
tizing the latent-CoT model to 4-bit weights and activa-
tions lowers ScienceQA accuracy from 79.97% to 11.16%,
and quantization-aware training recovers it only to 50.10%
(Fig. 1). We attribute this to the structure of latent rea-
soning rather than to tuning. In explicit CoT, a quantization
error perturbs a single token that is immediately re-encoded
as a discrete symbol. In latent CoT, continuous thoughts are
fed back as the next input embeddings, while their cached
keys remain accessible to subsequent queries. These path-
ways allow errors to propagate through later reasoning and

Two analyses support this account and determine our de-
sign. Operator ablations (Sec. 3.2) identify attention keys
as the dominant bottleneck: with linear layers at W4A4,
raising only the keys from 4 to 8 bits lifts accuracy from
11.16% to 70.35%, against at most 33.41% for queries, val-
ues, or attention probabilities. A perturbation analysis using
the total Jacobian of the answer logits through subsequent
latent steps further shows that predictive impact depends on
direction, not only magnitude: equal-norm perturbations of
continuous thoughts differ in induced KL by a median fac-
tor of 2.82, and a direction-aware estimate ranks them with
Spearman correlation 0.999 versus 0.270 for a magnitude-
based one (Fig. 4). Reconstruction error, the objective of
most quantizers, is therefore the wrong target; what must
be preserved is the information later steps retrieve and the
reasoning that consumes it.
We therefore introduce QL-VLM, a dynamics-aware
Quantization framework for low-bit Latent reasoning in
VLMs (Fig. 2), whose two components correspond to these
two findings. Dynamics-Guided Quantization (DGQ) quan-
tizes each rotary key pair jointly in polar coordinates with
a learned nonuniform codebook, so that the angular struc-
ture imposed by M-RoPE [22, 26] survives quantization,
and selects codewords under a prediction-sensitivity met-
ric rather than Euclidean distance, with codebooks grouped
by layer, head, rotary axis, key source, and the reasoning
step at which a latent key is created. Dynamics-Preserving
Distillation (DPD) trains the quantized student against a
full-precision teacher by aligning the information that la-
tent queries retrieve from multimodal tokens and earlier
thoughts, the continuous thoughts weighted by their esti-
mated downstream sensitivity, and the answer distribution,
so that the student’s reasoning trajectory, not only its output,
follows the teacher’s. Both vision and language backbones
are quantized.
On ScienceQA with Qwen2-VL-2B, QL-VLM reaches
90.43% at 4 bits and 94.20% at 8 bits, improving over
the matched-precision baseline by 40.33 and 7.49 points;
across three backbones and three datasets, the 4-bit gains
range from 21.37 to 48.39 points, and the quantized models
match or exceed full-precision latent CoT in eight of nine
settings. The 4-bit model generates 5.86 answer tokens on
average, shortening the decoding sequence by 4.75× rela-
tive to explicit CoT-SFT once the six thoughts are counted,
with 2.52× lower storage. Measured on an RTX PRO 6000
GPU, it answers in 0.50 s per question, against 3.20 s for
explicit CoT and 0.80 s for full-precision latent CoT.
2. Related Work
2.1. Efficient Chain-of-Thought
Efficient reasoning methods for Large-Language Models
(LLMs) can be grouped into input-based, output-based, and
model-based approaches [23].
Input-based methods en-
courage concise reasoning through prompting, as in Chain
of Draft [34], while prompt compression such as LLMLin-
gua [11] removes redundant input tokens.
Output-based
methods [5, 35] replace textual traces with latent represen-
tations: Coconut [8] reuses hidden representations as in-
puts, CODI [21] learns continuous thoughts through self-
distillation, and SIM-CoT [27] adds step-level supervi-
sion through a training-only decoder. Model-based meth-
ods optimize reasoning length through length-aware re-
wards or variable-length training data, exemplified by O1-
Pruner [16]. For VLMs, efficient reasoning must addition-
ally preserve visual evidence and cross-modal interactions.
FastV [2] prunes visual inputs, while Skywork R1V [18]
reduces excessive reasoning through adaptive-length distil-
lation. Heima [20] compresses multimodal rationales into
abstract thinking tokens, and CoLT [10] learns compact la-
tent thoughts through decoder-based and internal supervi-
sion. These methods primarily compress token sequences
while retaining backbone precision, leaving substantial stor-
age and arithmetic costs. How low-bit errors affect multi-
modal retrieval and subsequent latent reasoning remains in-
sufficiently characterized.

3. Methodology
QL-VLM combines low-bit quantization with multimodal
latent reasoning, using a full-precision reference to super-
vise the compressed model.
We introduce the prelimi-
naries in Sec. 3.1 and diagnose quantization sensitivity in
Sec. 3.2. This analysis motivates Dynamics-Guided Rotary
Key Quantization in Sec. 3.3 and Dynamics-Preserving La-
tent Reasoning Distillation in Sec. 3.4.

3.2. Quantization Sensitivity in Latent CoT
Operator sensitivity.
Fig. 3(a) shows that accuracy de-
creases from 79.97% to 73.49% with Linear-only quantiza-
tion and 40.16% with attention-operand-only quantization;
quantizing both yields 11.16%. With Linear layers fixed at
W4A4, increasing only K from four to eight bits achieves
70.35%, compared with 18.74%, 17.65%, and 33.41%
when increasing Q, V, and P, respectively (Fig. 3(b)). This
identifies key precision as a major bottleneck under the
tested protocol. A possible contributor is cached-key reuse
across reasoning steps, allowing key perturbations to affect
multiple subsequent reads and continuous thoughts. This
motivates using the answer logits to characterize perturba-
tion effects and derive a prediction-sensitive error metric.

5. Conclusion
We introduced QL-VLM for efficient latent reasoning,
addressing both reasoning length and the cost of low-
precision execution. Our diagnosis identifies key sensitivity
and direction-dependent predictive effects of continuous-
thought errors. Therefore, DGQ preserves rotary-key struc-
ture through nonuniform quantization, while DPD aligns
continuous thoughts, latent-query readouts, and predictions
with a full-precision teacher. Experiments across VLMs
and tasks show improved low-bit accuracy and cross-dataset
generalization, alongside compact reasoning, lower theoret-
ical storage, and less measured inference time.
