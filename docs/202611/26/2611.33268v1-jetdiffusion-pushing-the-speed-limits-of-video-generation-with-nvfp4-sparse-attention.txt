ABSTRACT
Attention over long sequences is a major computational bottleneck in video gen-
eration, which can be alleviated through sparsity and low-bit quantization. We
propose JetDiffusion, a video generation system built on trainable Video Sparse
Quantized Attention (VSQA), with custom inference kernels that combine native
NVFP4 computation and kernel fusion. At 90% sparsity, VSQA accelerates atten-
tion by 8–29x compared with FlashAttention on different hardwares. To further
accelerate video generation, we combine VSQA with NVFP4 linear quantization
and step distillation. Although each technique can individually preserve genera-

INTRODUCTION
Attention is a major computational bottleneck in video generation: higher resolution and longer
duration increase the number of spatiotemporal tokens, and the cost of full attention grows quadrat-
ically with it. Sparse attention (Zhang et al., 2025g; Xi et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026e; Li et al.,
2026b; Zhang et al., 2026b) and low-bit attention (Zhang et al., 2025c;a;b; 2026f) reduce this cost in
complementary ways: sparsity reduces the number of token interactions, while quantization lowers
the cost of the interactions that remain. However, combining high sparsity with 4-bit quantization
significantly perturb the pretrained model, causing compounding loss in generation quality.
Recent works apply post-training quantization to sparse attention without any training (Zhang et al.,
2025d; Zhao et al., 2026b), or adapt the model to sparsity and quantization jointly through train-
ing (Liu et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2026d). These efforts mainly target 8-bit attention. Native
NVFP4 support on NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs (Alvarez et al., 2025) makes 4-bit matrix multiplica-
tion substantially cheaper, offering an opportunity to further accelerate sparse attention. However, 4-
bit precision leaves far less room for error than 8-bit. With NVFP4, quantization error grows sharply,
and post-training calibration alone no longer preserves the quality of the pretrained model (Zhang
et al., 2026f). Moreover, quantizing attention breaks the identities that standard attention kernels
rely on in the backward pass, requiring dedicated kernels for trainable NVFP4 sparse attention.
We propose Video Sparse Quantized Attention (VSQA), to our knowledge the first trainable atten-
tion method that combines high sparsity with NVFP4 quantization. VSQA keeps the coarse-to-fine
structure of VSA (Zhang et al., 2026e), which pools tokens into cubes to select the cubes each query
attends to, and computes both QK⊤and PV of the selected token-level attention in NVFP4. The
inference kernels use native NVFP4 matrix multiplication, traverse only the selected cube pairs,
and fuse pooling with quantization so that non-matrix work stays small as sparsity increases. We
implement both training and inference kernels for the RTX 5090 and the GB300.
To achieve acceleration in the end-to-end system, we extend VSQA into JetDiffusion, a complete
system where we additionally quantize the linear layers to NVFP4 and distill the model to three
denoising steps. Realizing these compression techniques without sacrificing generation quality re-
quires adapting the model to the combined effects of low-precision computation, sparse attention,
and few-step denoising. To maximize speedup, our quantization setup operates without smoothing
tricks, making quantization error a central challenge for this adaptation.
Through empirical observations, we find that a dedicated reconstruction warm-up substantially sta-
bilizes subsequent training by reducing quantization error. While conventional QAT/QAD objectives
can reduce quantization error, our observations motivate a more targeted objective: layer-wise re-
construction, which directly matches quantized features to a frozen, unquantized reference (Li et al.,
2021). Following this warm-up, we enable sparse attention and proceed with ODE initialization
followed by DMD refinement, as in prior video distillation work (Yin et al., 2024b; 2025; Zhu et al.,
2026), retaining reconstruction regularization during ODE initialization to limit distribution drift.
We apply this recipe to Wan2.1-1.3B at 480p and Wan2.1-14B at 720p (Wan et al., 2025). At 90%
sparsity, VSQA accelerates attention on Wan2.1-14B by 8.0× over dense FlashAttention-4 (Zadouri
et al., 2026) on the GB300, and 28.5× over FlashAttention-2 on the RTX5090, much faster than
other implementations such as SageSLA (Zhang et al., 2025f). With NVFP4 linear layers and three-
step generation, JetDiffusion achieves 273× end-to-end speedup on Wan2.1-14B on the RTX 5090
and 119× on the GB300, with VBench scores that match or exceed those of the dense BF16 base
model.

RELATED WORK
Sparse and low-bit attention. Sparse attention reduces token interactions through structured pat-
terns or adaptive token and block selection (Zhang et al., 2025g; 2026e; Xi et al., 2025; Yang et al.,
2025; Xia et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026b; Li et al., 2026b;
Chen et al., 2026a; Sun et al., 2026; Tan et al., 2026; Zhan et al., 2025). Other methods compen-
sate for omitted interactions through auxiliary approximations or residual reconstruction (Zhang
et al., 2026c; Zhou et al., 2026; Taghavi et al., 2026). Low-bit attention accelerates computation
by reducing numerical precision (Zhang et al., 2025c; Chen et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025e), us-
ing techniques such as smoothing to mitigate quantization error (Zhang et al., 2025a;b; Li et al.,
2026c). Attn-QAT adapts models to 4-bit attention through quantization-aware training (Zhang
et al., 2026f), while SageBwd studies trainable 8-bit attention (Zhang et al., 2026a). Combining
sparsity and quantization, SpargeAttn and PAROAttention provide training-free approaches (Zhang
et al., 2025d; Zhao et al., 2026b), whereas FPSAttention and SLA2 use training to preserve qual-
ity (Liu et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2026d). To the best of our knowledge, VSQA is the first trainable
NVFP4 sparse attention method for video generation.
Few-step distillation. Few-step generation can be learned through consistency objectives (Song
et al., 2023; Lu & Song, 2025; Zheng et al., 2026) or distribution matching methods such as
Diff-Instruct and Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), which align student and teacher out-
put distributions (Luo et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2024b;a). For DMD in autoregressive video gener-
ation, CausVid initializes the student with teacher-generated ODE trajectories (Yin et al., 2025),
and Causal Forcing examines their compatibility with the student’s causal conditioning (Zhu et al.,
2026). Related work reduces train–test conditioning gaps using student-generated histories (Huang
et al., 2026a) and studies consistency-based initialization and its alignment with DMD refine-
ment (Zhao et al., 2026a; Li et al., 2026a). Inspired by these works, we use ODE initialization
to adapt the model to both few-step denoising and sparse attention before DMD refinement.
Combining compression and distillation. TurboDiffusion and LightWan2.2 combine step distilla-
tion, sparse attention, and quantization under FP8 (Zhang et al., 2025f; Lv et al., 2026). FastWan
jointly trains sparse attention and few-step generation with DMD (FastVideo Team, 2025). FastWan-
QAD applies quantization-aware fine-tuning and DMD with dense low-bit attention (FastVideo
Team, 2026), while SQuad adapts sub-quadratic attention through flow-matching fine-tuning be-
fore DMD2 (Karnewar et al., 2026). LongLive-2.0 incorporates NVFP4 into autoregressive training
and DMD (Chen et al., 2026b). VSQA targets ultrafast attention and end-to-end video generation
by combining NVFP4 sparse attention, linear quantization, and few-step distillation.

VSQA: VIDEO SPARSE QUANTIZED ATTENTION WITH NVFP4
VSQA builds on the coarse-to-fine attention structure of VSA (Zhang et al., 2026e). We retain its
cube-level routing and gated coarse branch, and apply NVFP4 quantization (Alvarez et al., 2025) to
the token-level sparse attention branch.

Details of kernel implementation.
Our inference kernels traverse only the selected cube pairs
and use native low-precision matrix multiplication for the fine branch. On Blackwell, reducing
non-matrix operations is important because their throughput scales more slowly than matrix mul-
tiplication (Zadouri et al., 2026). Pooling and input quantization still process all tokens, so their
overhead becomes more prominent as sparsity increases. We fuse Q/K/V pooling and quantization
around a shared input load: the loaded features supply both the unquantized routing inputs and the
packed NVFP4 operands, avoiding separate reads for the two operations.
On the GB300, the kernel computes QK⊤in NVFP4 and PV in FP8. As Attn-QAT reports (Zhang
et al., 2026f), packing FP4 probabilities and transferring their scales add overhead, so an NVFP4
value product is not the faster choice on this GPU. Appendix B compares videos generated with
the FP8 and NVFP4 value products and finds no visible difference, as for fake quantization above.
For an NVFP4 value product, the GB300 kernel also reduces the overhead of quantizing the atten-
tion probabilities. A direct implementation exponentiates the scores, finds a scale for each group of
probabilities, and rescales the probabilities before packing them. Because exponentiation preserves
ordering, the kernel instead finds each group’s maximum in the score domain and folds the rescal-
ing into the exponentiation, and it accumulates the softmax denominator from the same unrounded
values. This removes a separate reciprocal-and-rescaling pass.

4.1
OVERCOMING THE NVFP4 QUANTIZATION GAP
Aggressive 4-bit quantization first introduces a pronounced low-bit initialization gap. Compared
with milder 8-bit formats such as FP8, prior work has shown (Huang et al., 2026b; Li et al., 2026d;
2024; Wang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b) that full 4-bit weights and activations are more sensitive
to limited activation ranges, outliers, and timestep-dependent activation variations in diffusion mod-
els, resulting in larger quantization errors. Figure 2 (left) illustrates the challenge: direct DMD with
NVFP4 attains lower Quality and Total scores than the FP8 run, motivating additional adaptation
before distillation. To achieve extreme inference acceleration, we don’t want to apply any additional
compensation or techniques to the quantization process, which results in a larger initial perturbation
and makes DMD training less stable. We therefore need to adapt to the dominant low-bit quantiza-
tion errors before entering few-step distillation.
TAKEAWAY 1. NVFP4 introduces a large quantization perturbation that calls for a dedicated
warmup before few-step distillation.

TAKEAWAY 2. Recovering standalone quality with QAT or QAD does not ensure a suitable
DMD initialization, because teacher alignment can deteriorate.
Inspired by reconstruction-based quantization methods (Nagel et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Hubara
et al., 2021), which constrain quantization-induced deviations by reconstructing intermediate out-
puts at the layer or block level, we instead adopt fine-grained reconstruction supervision during
quantization warmup. As illustrated in Figure 3, layer reconstruction substantially better preserves
the layout and content of the original teacher generation throughout training.

than QAD. Since DMD serves as a refinement of an initialized few-step generator (Zhao et al.,
2026a), we prioritize preserving teacher alignment while reducing disruptive quantization errors to
provide a more stable starting point for subsequent quality refinement.

CONCLUSION
We presented VSQA, a trainable sparse attention method that combines high sparsity with native
NVFP4 computation for video diffusion. VSQA achieves a 8.0× attention speedup over dense
FlashAttention-4 at 90% sparsity on NVIDIA GB300 while preserving generation quality. Build-
ing on VSQA, we further presented JetDiffusion, which combines sparse NVFP4 attention with
NVFP4 linear layers and few-step distillation to accelerate the full video generation pipeline. To-
gether, these techniques enable 273× end-to-end acceleration on Wan2.1-14B on the RTX 5090,
demonstrating the potential of jointly exploiting sparsity, low precision, and generation-step reduc-
tion for efficient video generation.

TRAINING SETUP

dense NVFP4 student with the layer reconstruction loss on cached teacher states. The sparse ini-

trajectories (48 teacher steps, flow shift 8), with the ODE endpoint loss and the reconstruction reg-
ularizer weighted equally (λ = 1). DMD refines the result. Every warmup stage uses an effective
batch size of 16 and a learning rate of 10−5, and we take its checkpoint after 500 updates. Table 4
lists the DMD hyperparameters. All DMD runs in Table 1 had converged when training ended, and
only then were their checkpoints evaluated.
