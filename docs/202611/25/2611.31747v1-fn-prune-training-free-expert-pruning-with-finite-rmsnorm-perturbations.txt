# FN-Prune: Training-Free Expert Pruning with Finite RMSNorm Perturbations 

Zeyan Li 1 Jianfeng Xu 1

## Abstract 

Expert pruning reduces the memory footprint of mixture-of-experts language models, but retaining model quality depends on which experts are re-moved. Frequency- and magnitude-based scores measure the expert in isolation and overlook how normalization consumes its contribution: sub-tracting one expert changes both the direction of the residual stream and the scale applied to what remains. We propose FN-Prune, a training-free criterion that compares the next RMSNorm outputs before and after subtracting each router-weighted expert contribution, holding original routes and weights fixed. Squared differences are averaged over all calibration tokens in one pass; low-scoring experts are physically deleted at uniform per-layer ratios, and inference reroutes among survivors. At 50% expert removal, FN-Prune achieves lower WikiText-2 perplexity than the best published methods on Qwen3, ERNIE, and OLMoE, including a 32% reduction on OL-MoE. At the same budget, GSM8K accuracy im-proves over the best published methods by 7.5

and 7.0 percentage points on Qwen3 and ERNIE, respectively, while multiple-choice gains remain task-dependent.

Any such decision implicitly ranks experts by how much their removal would cost the model. Existing rankings esti-mate that cost from the expert alone: usage frequency counts selections (Lu et al., 2024b; Zhou et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2025), gate mass weights them, and output norms describe what the expert computes (Li et al., 2024; Omi et al., 2025). A rarely selected expert can carry substantial gate mass, and a frequently selected one can produce small outputs—but all of these signals are measured upstream of the opera-tion that actually consumes the contribution. In the blocks studied here, that consumer is normalization: each expert output is added to a residual stream that the next RMSNorm rescales. Removing one contribution therefore changes both the direction of that stream and the scale applied to ev-erything left in it, even when the other contributions are unchanged. A contribution aligned with the residual mostly changes a magnitude that normalization absorbs; an equally large contribution in another direction changes where the model looks (Fig. 1). The cost of removing an expert is thus contextual—jointly determined by its contribution and the residual state that contribution enters—and is not readable from the contribution’s own frequency or magnitude. ## 2. Related Work 

Compressing mixture-of-experts models clusters along three axes: the signal for ranking experts, the reuse of retained capacity, and the allocation of removals across layers. The subsections follow this order; mixed-precision and dynamic pruning target complementary costs (Huang et al., 2025a). 

2.1. Ranking Experts for Removal 

How often an expert is selected predicts removal damage well enough that frequency pruning yields smooth quality curves at moderate keep ratios (Lu et al., 2024b), and router probabilities and gate activations refine the same participa-tion signal (Zhou et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2025). A second family looks at what the expert computes: weighting out-put norms by router gates targets MoE-layer reconstruction error (Lasby et al., 2026), and similarity between experts in-dicates which ones can be consolidated (Li et al., 2024; Omi et al., 2025). Both families, however, measure the expert in isolation—its participation statistics or the magnitude of its output. The criterion of this paper instead scores the change that a removal induces in the normalized state the next block actually reads, which brings the residual context and the recomputed scale into the ranking. A more recent line is explicitly residual-aware: each cali-bration token is weighted by how much the routed block changes the residual direction, and experts are scored under that weighting in one pass from a small in-domain cali-bration set (Dong et al., 2025). That token-level weight is shared by every expert the token selects, whereas the fi-nite comparison used here is specific to the expert whose contribution is removed and is averaged over the complete calibration corpus. Removal decisions have also borrowed from reconstruction-based dense pruning (Frantar et al., 2022) and from subset search, whether by evolutionary optimization over deletion plans (Liu et al., 2024) or by marginal-contribution estimation through Monte Carlo coali-tion evaluation with early truncation and router-guided sam-pling (Huang et al., 2025b); the latter’s preservation guaran-tee assumes additive utility and bounded estimation error, and all of these routes require repeated evaluations or proxy objectives, while both states scored here come from a sin-gle fixed-routing forward pass. Selection can also protect specific capabilities, safety in particular (Lai et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2025); parallel work characterizes expert roles through task, semantic, and geometric lenses (Olson et al., 2025; Herbst et al., 2026; Ternovtsii & Bilak, 2026; Ye et al., 2026). 

2.2. Reusing and Retraining Capacity 

Rather than deleting experts, one can reuse their capac-ity. Merging consolidates similar experts (Li et al., 2024), 2FN-Prune: Finite RMSNorm Perturbations 

absorption transfers routing mass between them (Aquino-Michaels, 2026), and related operations preserve pairwise similarity during pruning (Omi et al., 2025) or expose redun-dancy through specialization (Guo et al., 2025). Replacing experts by learned constant modules (Zhang et al., 2026) and adjusting merged models with recovery training change what the retained parameters compute rather than which pa-rameters remain. We compare these operations at matched routed-expert budgets, while the matched-quota frequency control isolates what the ranking alone contributes. 

2.3. Distributing Removals across Layers 

How many experts each layer can spare is independent of which ones. In dense-model pruning, reconstruction-optimal updates (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023), activation-weighted magnitude (Sun et al., 2023), and structured group pruning (Ma et al., 2023) operate under shared ratios, whereas allocation by outlier structure (Yin et al., 2024), by heavy-tailed weight statistics (Lu et al., 2024a), or learned jointly with width and depth (Xia et al., 2023) redistributes them. MoE models behave the same way in our prelimi-nary measurements: reallocating matched removals changes perplexity substantially (Section 4.3). 

## 3. FN-Prune 

3.1. Method Overview 

FN-Prune ranks experts by the change their removal would induce at the next RMSNorm. For the same token and na-tive routing, we subtract one expert’s weighted contribution from the residual and evaluate RMSNorm at both states. Av-eraging the squared differences over the calibration corpus gives one score per expert. Removals use exact uniform per-layer quotas (Figure 2).

## 4. Experiments 

Our evaluation is organized around four questions: • RQ1: Published baselines. How does FN-Prune com-pare with published compression methods at matched budgets? • RQ2: Design choices. Which scoring, aggregation and allocation choices matter, and does the selected score beat routing frequency under matched deletion? • RQ3: Capability retention. Which downstream capa-bilities are retained after pruning? • RQ4: Physical efficiency. Do physical deletions re-duce model size and improve throughput? 

4.1. Experimental Setup Models and budgets We study Qwen3-30B-A3B (Yang et al., 2025a), ERNIE-4.5-21B-A3B-PT (Baidu ERNIE Team, 2025), and OLMoE-1B-7B-0924 (Muennighoff et al., 2024). Their routed-expert pools contain 48 × 128 = 6 ,144 ,

27 × 64 = 1 ,728 , and 16 × 64 = 1 ,024 slots, respectively. The target reductions are 1,536 , 432 , and 256 routed-expert slots at 25% , and 3,072 , 864 , and 512 at 50% . ERNIE’s shared experts are preserved. All methods match the num-ber of routed-expert slots while retaining their native com-pression operation: deletion, novice replacement, merging, or merging with recovery training. For novice replacement, the budget counts experts replaced by constant modules. FN-Prune scores with the checkpoint’s native selection rule, routing weights and correction bias, then applies that policy to the surviving experts after deletion. 

Calibration and development Calibration uses the com-plete WikiText-2 raw training split; development, where needed, uses the complete validation split; and token-weighted perplexity uses the complete test split (Merity et al., 2017). Tokenized streams are packed at context length 

2,048 , retaining the final, possibly partial chunk. Search and recovery methods use their published search or training stages. 

Task evaluation We report zero-shot accuracy on MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021) and WinoGrande (Sak-aguchi et al., 2020), and zero-shot accuracy with character-normalized continuation scores on OpenBookQA (Mihaylov et al., 2018). GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021) uses five-shot greedy decoding and exact match with the evaluator’s 

strict-match answer filter. The three-task mean is the equal-weight mean of MMLU, OpenBookQA, and Wino-Grande accuracy; perplexity and GSM8K are reported sepa-rately. Qwen3 reasoning is disabled and no chat template is applied. Every configuration uses seed 0 and BF16 infer-ence, with one run on each complete benchmark population (MMLU 14 ,042 ; OpenBookQA 500 ; WinoGrande 1,267 ;GSM8K 1,319 ; WikiText-2 test 4,358 source rows). All win counts refer to these five metrics across the six model– budget cells ( 30 conditions).

## 5. Conclusion and Limitations 

FN-Prune turns the finite normalized effect of removing one routed contribution into a one-pass criterion for physical expert deletion. At 50% removal, it improves WikiText-2 perplexity across all three families and GSM8K on Qwen3 and ERNIE relative to the strongest published methods. The task profiles locate its main benefit in aggressive language-model compression, with selective downstream retention. The current evidence covers one calibration corpus, seed 

0, and the tested RMSNorm architectures; confirmatory evaluation on held-out tasks, alternative calibration domains, and multiple seeds would strengthen the conclusions. The local score fixes routing while deployment reroutes, leaving joint selection and decision-aware objectives as the main extensions. The throughput profiles support within-family trends, while serving latency and statistical significance require dedicated evaluation. 8FN-Prune: Finite RMSNorm Perturbations 

## Impact Statement 

Reducing model memory can make existing language mod-els more accessible, but it does not establish that their out-puts are more reliable. Our results show that pruning can im-prove perplexity relative to other compressed models while downstream retention remains task-dependent. Compressed models should therefore be evaluated on their intended tasks before deployment, especially where errors have consequen-tial effects. We do not assess safety, fairness, or real-world reliability, and the compression results should not be inter-preted as evidence for those properties.
