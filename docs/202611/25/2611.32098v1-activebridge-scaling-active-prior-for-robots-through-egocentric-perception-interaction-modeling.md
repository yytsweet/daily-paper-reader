## 摘要
机器人基础模型现已广泛用于操作任务，而第一人称视频为规模化提供了丰富数据。然而，现有方法忽视了第一人称视频中捕获的人类主动先验，这限制了其在非结构化场景中的性能。它们基于给定观测行动，而不是像人类那样主动寻求信息。我们提出 ActiveBridge，一个训练框架，通过学习这些人类主动先验，并通过世界-动作建模将其迁移到机器人。我们设计了一种机器人，通过头部动作主动控制其视点，仅使用单个头部相机（无需腕部相机）获取操作所需的视觉信息。在人类训练过程中，一个以指令为条件的模型联合预测未来的第一人称视频和同步的头-腕动作轨迹，其中头部动作为主动感知提供显式监督。实验表明，ActiveBridge 在主动操作任务上优于基线，消融实验证明了联合头-腕动作监督的益处。

## Abstract
Robotics foundation models are now widely used for manipulation, and egocentric videos offer rich data for scaling. However, existing methods overlook the human active priors captured in egocentric videos, which limits performance in unstructured scenes. They act on given observations instead of actively seeking information as humans do. We propose ActiveBridge, a training framework that learns these human active priors and transfers them to robots through world-action modeling. We design a robot that actively controls its viewpoint through head actions to acquire the visual information needed for manipulation using a single head camera, without wrist cameras. During human training, an instruction-conditioned model jointly predicts future egocentric videos and synchronized head-wrist action trajectories, with head actions providing explicit supervision for active perception. Experiments show that ActiveBridge outperforms baselines on active manipulation tasks, while ablations demonstrate the benefit of joint head-wrist action supervision.
