# Two-Stage Post Training for Flow Based Embodied Models via Multi-Teacher On Policy Distillation 

Jiaming Zhang 1 Yao Lyu 2 Haoning Wang 1 Yongjun Chen 1 Liping Zhang 1 Shengbo Eben Li 3

## Abstract 

Large-scale pretraining provides embodied mod-els with general capabilities, but reliable task exe-cution still requires post training. In multitask set-tings, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) faces distribu-tion mismatch between training and deployment, whereas reinforcement learning (RL) is limited by sparse rewards and uneven learning progress across tasks. Multi-teacher on policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates expert capabilities through dense supervision on the model’s own trajectories, but achieving performance beyond the teachers remains challenging. We propose a two-stage post training framework for flow-based embodied models, constructing distillation objectives from the velocity regression formulation of conditional flow matching. Stage 1 efficiently consolidates expert capabilities through MOPD. Stage 2 com-bines RL with teacher distillation that weights velocity errors according to the sampling noise, where distillation is restricted to failed trajectories to supplement sparse rewards with dense guidance without directly constraining successful behaviors. Experiments across multiple models and bench-marks show that the framework outperforms joint SFT and exceeds the corresponding teachers’ ag-gregate success rates. Sample efficiency compar-isons show higher success rates than direct RL with fewer environment interactions.

For example, vision-language-action (VLA) models generate actions from visual observations, language instructions and propriocep-tive states (Black et al., 2025b), while world action mod-els (WAMs) jointly predict future observations and actions (Ye et al., 2026b). Despite their broad capabilities, these models often require post training to achieve reliable per-formance on target tasks. Maintaining a separate policy for each task incurs storage and maintenance costs, along with the overhead of switching between models during deploy-ment (Du & Lin, 2026; Adamopoulos et al., 2026). These costs increase as the task set grows, motivating post training methods that improve multitask performance within a single shared policy. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) are two common approaches to post training. SFT trains models on expert demonstrations, using observations and task conditions as inputs and deriving supervision from expert actions (Black et al., 2025b). RL instead uses trajec-tories collected through the model’s own interaction with the environment to optimize expected cumulative reward (Chen et al., 2026a). These approaches have complementary strengths and limitations. SFT provides dense supervision that supports efficient task learning, but its effectiveness depends on demonstration quality and coverage. During ex-ecution, the model may encounter states poorly represented in the demonstrations, limiting its ability to generalize be-yond the demonstrated behavior (Ross et al., 2011). RL can mitigate this distribution mismatch by learning from states visited by the model itself. However, sparse rewards make it difficult to determine how individual actions con-tribute to task outcomes, complicating credit assignment and reducing learning efficiency (Lu et al., 2026). In multi-task settings, SFT requires demonstrations covering a wider range of tasks, increasing data collection costs (Khazatsky et al., 2024), while uneven learning progress and imbalanced reward feedback across tasks complicate joint optimization for RL (Kalashnikov et al., 2022). On policy distillation (OPD) provides such guidance through trained teacher models that supervise learning on trajectories generated by the model being optimized. In large language models (LLMs), OPD trains the model to match the teacher’s next-token predictive distribution at each position in its own generated sequences. Multi-teacher on policy distillation (MOPD) extends this approach by selecting specialized teachers for different tasks and con-solidating their capabilities into a shared model. It has become an important paradigm for capability integration during LLM post training (LLM-Core Xiaomi, 2026; Kimi Team, 2026; DeepSeek-AI, 2026). By providing dense su-pervision throughout generated trajectories, OPD can im-prove sample efficiency and reduce training costs compared with sparse-reward RL (Qwen Team, 2025; Zhong et al., 2026). However, consolidating multiple experts does not guarantee that the resulting model matches each teacher on its respective tasks. Different teachers can induce conflict-ing updates in shared parameters, improving performance on some tasks at the expense of others (Tian et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). Moreover, matching teacher predictions does not directly optimize task success, so reducing distillation error need not improve closed-loop performance. These lim-itations motivate combining environmental feedback with teacher supervision to further improve task performance after consolidating expert capabilities. For flow-based embodied models, teacher supervision must also account for the continuous action generation process. Unlike autoregressive models that generate discrete tokens, these models generate continuous action chunks by integrat-ing a learned velocity field (Lipman et al., 2023). Diffu-sionOPD introduces stochasticity through SDE sampling to enable teacher supervision via closed-form KL minimiza-tion between Gaussian transitions (Li et al., 2026b). Recent work extends this approach to flow-based VLAs, consolidat-ing expert capabilities through teacher supervision on noisy actions along the denoising trajectories (Ni et al., 2026; Lv et al., 2026). Building on these advances, we seek to improve task performance beyond capability consolidation. Environmental rewards guide learning toward task success, but sparse feedback provides limited guidance for individual actions. Meanwhile, applying teacher supervision to all tra-jectories may unnecessarily constrain successful behaviors learned by the model. We therefore propose a two-stage post training framework that combines multi-teacher on policy distillation with policy optimization driven by en-vironmental rewards. The distillation objectives in both stages follow the velocity regression formulation of native conditional flow matching training. The first stage uses MOPD to efficiently integrate expert capabilities. The sec-ond stage combines RL with teacher supervision restricted to failed trajectories, providing dense guidance alongside environmental rewards without directly constraining suc-cessful behaviors. Our main contributions are as follows: • We propose a two-stage post training framework for flow-based embodied models. It combines efficient expert capability consolidation through MOPD with further task improvement through RL and selective teacher guidance, enabling a shared model to acquire and refine multitask capabilities. • We formulate distillation objectives based on the ve-locity regression principle of native conditional flow matching. The first stage matches teacher velocity predictions on the model’s own noisy actions. In the second stage, we adapt this objective to the transition dynamics and noise scale of stochastic flow sampling and combine it with RL. Teacher supervision is re-stricted to failed trajectories, providing dense guidance alongside sparse rewards without directly constraining successful behaviors. • Experiments across multiple embodied models and benchmarks demonstrate improvements in multitask success rates and sample efficiency. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. On Policy Distillation in LLM Post-Training 

OPD matches teacher and student next-token distributions on sequences generated by the student, providing dense supervision on the contexts encountered during generation (Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024). It has become an im-portant paradigm in LLM post training. For integrating spe-cialized capabilities, DeepSeek-V4 minimizes reverse KL over the full vocabulary (DeepSeek-AI, 2026), while Kimi K3 selects teachers by task domain and reasoning effort (Kimi Team, 2026). MiMo-V2-Flash combines token-level teacher signals with outcome rewards (LLM-Core Xiaomi, 2026). OPD is also used to recover capabilities across train-ing stages. GLM-5 uses earlier SFT and RL checkpoints as teachers to recover capabilities lost during subsequent train-ing (GLM-5 Team, 2026). Nemotron-Cascade 2 integrates multidomain OPD into its cascade RL pipeline, combining the strengths of intermediate checkpoints before continuing RL (Yang et al., 2026). OPD can also improve training efficiency. In a comparison from the same 8B checkpoint, Qwen3 achieves better math and coding performance with OPD than with RL, using about one-tenth of the GPU hours (Qwen Team, 2025). Nemotron-Cascade 2 likewise reports comparable or better scores on two ArenaHard v2 evalu-ations with fewer training steps than RLHF (Yang et al., 2026). 2Two-Stage Post-Training for Flow-Based Embodied Foundation Models 

2.2. Reinforcement Learning for Embodied Models 

RL is increasingly used for embodied post training to im-prove task performance through environment interaction. iRe-VLA alternates RL with supervised learning to bal-ance exploration and training stability (Guo et al., 2025). SimpleVLA-RL demonstrates scalable VLA fine-tuning through parallel environment interaction and enhanced ex-ploration under binary task rewards (Li et al., 2026a). For flow-based models, the lack of tractable action likeli-hoods poses a central challenge for policy gradient opti-mization. ReinFlow uses a learned noise model to con-struct stochastic transitions with tractable probabilities (Zhang et al., 2025). πRL studies this formulation alongside ODE-to-SDE conversion for VLA fine-tuning (Chen et al., 2026a). Flow policy optimization (FPO) instead derives a likelihood-ratio surrogate from conditional flow matching losses (McAllister et al., 2026). Lyu et al. adopt a related sur-rogate to optimize a latent action policy with a frozen VLA decoder (Lyu et al., 2026). Value estimates provide another source of guidance for policy improvement. Q-VGM uses action-value gradients to construct velocity targets at late denoising steps, updating the VLA action expert through local velocity matching (Wang et al., 2026b). To reduce the cost of adapting pretrained generative models, DSRL opti-mizes a policy over input noise to guide action generation while keeping the base model frozen (Wagenmaker et al., 2025). 

2.3. On Policy Distillation for Continuous Generative Models 

In continuous generative models, OPD incorporates teacher supervision into iterative denoising by querying teachers along the student’s sampling trajectories. DiffusionOPD ex-tends the conditional distribution matching objective of au-toregressive OPD to Markov chains over continuous states. It directly minimizes closed-form reverse KL between Gaus-sian transitions with shared covariance under SDE sampling and uses one-step transition matching under deterministic sampling (Li et al., 2026b). DanceOPD represents expert capabilities as velocity fields and routes each sample to a single field for supervision at a low-noise point on the student trajectory (Zhou et al., 2026). FlowCTS compares teacher and student continuations from the same point on a student trajectory. An upper bound on their discrepancy yields a temporally weighted velocity matching objective that supports both single-point and multistep supervision (Ye et al., 2026a). Similar approaches have been applied to embodied mod-els. Ni et al. use teacher velocity supervision to consolidate experts from LIBERO, CALVIN, and Meta-World into a single VLA (Ni et al., 2026). VLA-MOPD adopts a simi-lar approach to consolidate expertise across suites or tasks within individual benchmarks, with additional evaluation on a real robot (Lv et al., 2026). REPAIR instead queries local RL specialists for candidate actions at critical task phases. During on policy refinement, it weights these candidates using relative critic scores and transfers expert behavior through a weighted flow matching objective (Jiang et al., 2026).

## 4. Two-Stage Post Training with Multi-Teacher Distillation 

4.1. Framework Overview 

As shown in Figure 1, we propose a two-stage post train-ing framework for multitask flow-based embodied models. The first stage uses MOPD to efficiently consolidate expert capabilities into a shared model. Teachers provide dense supervision on observations collected through the current model’s environment interactions and noisy actions along its denoising trajectories. The second stage combines RL with teacher distillation on failed trajectories, supplement-ing sparse rewards with dense guidance without directly constraining successful behaviors. 

4.2. Stage 1: Multi-Teacher OPD

4.3. Stage 2: Teacher-Guided RL 

The first stage efficiently consolidates expert capabilities, but reducing distillation error does not necessarily improve task success. Supervision from different teachers may also induce conflicting updates in shared parameters, limiting the performance achievable through distillation alone. The second stage therefore introduces RL to optimize task perfor-mance using environmental rewards. We retain teacher dis-tillation on failed trajectories to supplement sparse rewards with dense guidance, while leaving successful behaviors free of direct teacher constraints. We use the Flow-SDE sampler introduced in Section 3.2 for stochastic action generation. Teacher distillation retains the velocity regression principle of the first stage, with super-vision adjusted to the stochastic transition scale. Velocity predictions determine the transition mean through the drift, while the noise magnitude determines the scale of varia-tion around that mean. ## 5. Experiments 

5.1. Experimental Setup 

We conduct experiments with three flow-based embodied models, GR00T N1.5 (NVIDIA et al., 2025), QwenVLA (Wang et al., 2026a), and π0.5 (Black et al., 2025a), across LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023), RoboCasa Kitchen (Nasiriany et al., 2024), and RoboCasa GR1 (NVIDIA et al., 2025). LIBERO comprises four suites: Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long. For RoboCasa, we group tasks by similarity. Kitchen comprises PnP, Articulated, and Appliance–Coffee, with eight tasks each. These cover pick-and-place, door and drawer manipulation, and appliance operation. GR1 com-prises Articulated, Tableware, and Storage, with six, nine, and nine tasks. These cover placing objects and closing articulated parts, transfers involving tableware and cook-ware, and storage. Within a benchmark, we train one shared model to handle all suites or task groups. Task partitions for RoboCasa and key training hyperparameters are provided in Appendix A. Each model is initialized from a multitask SFT checkpoint for the corresponding benchmark. Each task group has a teacher model trained through SFT or RL on its tasks. Teachers remain frozen during post training and are selected according to the task group. We freeze the vision-language backbone and fine-tune the action generation module, in-cluding the action expert and associated input and output projection layers. In the second stage, we also train a value network. Training uses eight NVIDIA H100 GPUs with 80 GB of memory each. ## 6. Conclusion 

We propose a two-stage framework for post training flow-based embodied models via multi-teacher on policy dis-tillation. The distillation objectives in both stages follow the velocity regression formulation of native conditional flow matching. Stage 1 uses MOPD to efficiently consoli-date expert capabilities. Stage 2 combines RL with teacher distillation adapted to the transition and noise scales of stochastic flow sampling. Teacher guidance is restricted to failed trajectories, supplementing sparse rewards with dense supervision without directly constraining success-ful behaviors. Experiments across three embodied models and benchmarks show that the framework improves mean success rates across tasks and surpasses the aggregate per-formance of the corresponding teachers. Ablations highlight the benefits of the two-stage design and failure-gated dis-tillation. Sample efficiency comparisons further show that the framework achieves higher success rates than direct RL with fewer environment interactions.
