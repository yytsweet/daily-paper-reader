Adaptive Action Horizon Selection via Flow-Field Uncertainty
Idan Ben-Ami1, Arnon Netzer1, Sarah Keren2, Idit Diamant1
1Arm AI Research (AAIR), Arm Ltd
2Technion Israel Institute of Technology
Abstract—Reliable uncertainty estimates are important for
adaptive execution in vision-language-action (VLA) models. Ex-
isting approaches such as Adaptive Action Chunking (AAC)
estimate action entropy from multiple sampled action chunks,
introducing additional inference cost and capturing both uncer-
tainty within an action trajectory and diversity across alternative
strategies. We introduce Divergence-based Uncertainty-aware
Adaptive Action Chunking (DUAC), a post-hoc method that
extracts trajectory-conditioned predictive uncertainty from the
vector field of a pretrained VLA. Building on a closed-form
flow-posterior covariance formulation, DUAC derives action-level
uncertainty scores from a single generated chunk using Jacobian–
vector products, without retraining or multiple endpoint samples.
DUAC strongly correlates with AAC, while capturing distinct
information: AAC measures global dispersion across sampled
chunks, whereas DUAC measures local uncertainty around the
trajectory selected for execution. Consequently, DUAC is less
sensitive to diversity among distinct but valid action strate-
gies. Across LIBERO, LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO-Plus, and Robo-
Casa, DUAC-based adaptive horizon selection performs favorably
among the evaluated methods, combining strong task success
with low uncertainty-estimation latency. These results establish
the flow vector field as an efficient, trajectory-conditioned source
of predictive uncertainty for robot decision making.
I. INTRODUCTION
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a
general approach to robotic manipulation, mapping visual
observations, language instructions, and robot state into ex-
ecutable actions [1]–[6]. Many recent VLAs generate con-
tinuous action chunks with diffusion or flow-matching ac-
tion heads [4], [7]–[13]. Starting from noise, these policies
repeatedly evaluate a learned vector field to transform a
latent variable into a coherent action trajectory. Despite the
inherently iterative nature of this computation, the internal flow
trajectory is usually treated only as a mechanism for producing
the final action, rather than as a source of information about
the reliability of that action.
Reliable estimates of predictive action uncertainty are par-
ticularly useful when action chunks are executed in closed
loop [14]–[16]. Longer execution horizons preserve temporal
consistency and reduce the frequency of policy inference,
but limit the policy’s responsiveness to new observations.
Shorter horizons permit frequent replanning, but can produce
discontinuous or overly reactive behavior. Adaptive Action
Chunking (AAC) [15] addresses this trade-off by sampling
multiple candidate action chunks, estimating the entropy of
the resulting action distribution at each predicted step, and
shortening the execution horizon when this entropy increases.
While effective, the quality and stability of its estimate depend

These limitations motivate using the local geometry of a
flow-matching policy to estimate predictive action uncertainty
without sampling multiple terminal action chunks. Xing et
al. [17] derive a closed-form expression that relates the condi-
tional posterior covariance of a flow-matching model to the di-
vergence of its learned vector field. However, the utility of this
formulation for VLA action generation remains unexplored,
including whether it can provide uncertainty for individual
actions within a predicted chunk and support adaptive action
horizon execution.
We introduce Divergence-based Uncertainty-aware Adap-
tive Action Chunking, DUAC , a post-hoc method that extracts
predictive uncertainty from the vector field of a pretrained
flow-matching VLA. We adapt the flow-posterior covariance
formulation to VLA policies and derive an action-wise uncer-
tainty signal for generated action chunks. At an intermediate
state of the flow process, DUAC estimates the dispersion of
terminal actions associated with that local flow state. When
nearby action trajectories converge toward similar terminal
actions, the prediction is more certain. When they remain dis-
persed, the terminal action is less determined. Unlike entropy-
based uncertainty in AAC [15], which summarizes variability
across independently sampled action chunks, our score is
conditioned on the intermediate state of the specific trajectory
being generated. It therefore provides a local, trajectory-
specific uncertainty estimate for the action chunk selected

We compare DUAC with the sampling-based action entropy

Nevertheless, the two methods capture different informa-
tion: AAC measures global dispersion across sampled action

DUAC is less sensitive to variability among distinct but valid
action strategies and avoids treating such diversity as uncer-

Plus, LIBERO-Pro, and RoboCasa, DUAC improves per-

latency relative to entropy-based AAC. Our contributions are
summarized as follows:
• We introduce Divergence-based Uncertainty-aware Adap-
tive Action Chunking (DUAC), which adapts the closed-
form flow-posterior covariance to VLA action generation
and derives per-action uncertainty scores from one gen-
erated action chunk using Jacobian–vector products.
• We provide a sampling-efficient alternative to entropy-
based AAC that requires one generated action chunk
and achieves substantially lower uncertainty-estimation
latency in our implementation.
• Since DUAC captures local uncertainty around the se-
lected action trajectory, we show that it is less sensitive
than AAC’s entropy to variability among distinct but valid
action strategies, reducing unnecessary replanning.
• We apply DUAC to adaptive horizon selection, achieving
improved performance over the evaluated methods.
II. METHOD
Our method extracts predictive uncertainty from an action
chunk generated by a flow-matching policy. We build on
the recent work of Xing et al. [17], who show that the
predictive uncertainty of a flow-matching model is encoded in
the local geometry of its learned velocity field. We adapt this
formulation to derive per-action uncertainty scores, yielding
an uncertainty profile over the prediction horizon. We use
this profile to determine how many actions to execute before
replanning. Figure 2 summarizes the uncertainty estimation
and its use for adaptive action execution. The method requires
no additional training or multiple action samples.

D. Relation to sampling-based uncertainty
DUAC and AAC measure related but distinct uncertainty
quantities. AAC estimates marginal action entropy from
the sample covariance of multiple generated action chunks,
whereas DUAC estimates predictive covariance conditioned
on an intermediate state of one generated trajectory. Under the
linear Gaussian flow interpolation, the law of total covariance
decomposes the marginal action covariance into the expected
trajectory-conditioned covariance and the variability of the
conditional means across trajectories. DUAC captures the
former component and is therefore less sensitive to diversity
among distinct but valid action strategies. We provide the
complete derivation in Appendix C.

III. EXPERIMENTS
Our experiments address the following questions: (i) Can
DUAC achieve competitive or improved task success while
reducing the latency and computational cost of adaptive ac-
tion execution? (ii) What information does DUAC which is
local, trajectory-specific divergence capture compared with the
global, distribution-level uncertainty measured by AAC [15],
particularly under distribution shift?
A. Experimental Setup
We evaluate publicly available π0.5 policies [11] on stan-
dard LIBERO, LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboCasa
Kitchen-24. We compare DUAC with fixed-horizon execu-
tion, AutoHorizon [16], and entropy-based AAC [15]. All
uncertainty estimates are computed from pretrained policies
at inference time, without retraining. Appendix A provides
complete benchmark and evaluation details.
B. Performance comparison
We investigate whether divergence-based uncertainty pro-
vides a signal for adaptive action execution. Following
AAC [15], the execution horizon is selected from the per-step
uncertainty profile. The action prefix is executed before the
policy replans from a new observation. Because this decision
applies to the specific actions about to be executed, we
evaluate the profile at a late flow time, when the trajectory is
largely formed but retains nonzero residual uncertainty. We use
τ = 0.9, which shows the strongest agreement with entropy-
based uncertainty in supplementary Figure 6.

IV. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
Our results show that the vector field of a pretrained flow-
matching VLA provides a useful trajectory-conditioned uncer-
tainty signal. Unlike AAC method, which measures dispersion
across multiple sampled action chunks, DUAC estimates the
uncertainty remaining around the particular trajectory selected
for execution. The two measures exhibit strong agreement at
late flow times, but differ when multiple valid strategies exist:
marginal entropy increases with diversity across strategies,
whereas DUAC more selectively characterizes the chosen
trajectory. When used for adaptive horizon selection, DUAC
achieves the highest observed aggregate success rates in our
evaluation and reduces uncertainty-estimation latency relative
to Entropy AAC.

B. Related Work
Flow-Based Action Generation in VLAs. Recent VLAs
[10]–[12] use conditional generative action heads to jointly
synthesize continuous action chunks rather than predicting
each action independently. π0 [10] and π0.5
[11] couples
a pretrained vision-language backbone with a flow-matching
action expert that transforms noise into a multi-step motor
trajectory conditioned on visual observations, language in-
structions, and robot state. Similarly, GR00T
[12] uses a
diffusion-transformer action module, conditioned on a vision-
language representation, to generate chunked controls for
heterogeneous robot embodiments. By modeling the future
actions jointly, these generative heads capture cross-timestep
dependencies and multimodal action distributions, yielding
temporally coherent trajectories suitable for dexterous control.
Uncertainty-Guided VLA. Recent work estimates VLA
uncertainty to anticipate failures, detect distribution shift,
guide data acquisition, and support runtime control
[14]–
[16], [26]–[28]. R¨omer et al. [26] quantify epistemic un-
certainty through velocity-field disagreement across an en-
semble of flow-matching policies, using the resulting signal
for deployment-time failure detection and uncertainty-guided
active fine-tuning. ReconVLA [14] applies anomaly detec-
tion in the robot state space to identify unsafe or out-of-
distribution states before failure. Beyond failure awareness,
uncertainty has also been used to determine how long a
predicted action chunk should be executed before replanning.
AutoHorizon [16] avoids probabilistic uncertainty estimation
and instead uses action self-attention as a proxy for the policy’s
predictive limit. AAC [15] estimates per-step action entropy
from multiple sampled chunks and selects the execution hori-
zon at the sharpest increase in uncertainty. Existing approaches
provide actionable reliability signals but often require repeated
sampling, policy ensembles, or auxiliary learned components.
Our method instead derives uncertainty directly from the local
geometry of the learned vector field along a single generated
trajectory, requiring neither additional training nor multiple
models or action samples.
C. Background and Derivations
Flow Matching. Flow matching learns how to transform a
simple noise distribution into a data distribution. We use the
standard linear interpolation [18]
xτ = (1 −τ)x0 + τx1,
(9)
where x0 ∼N(0, I) is Gaussian noise, x1 ∼pdata(· | c) is
a data sample, and τ ∈[0, 1] denotes flow time. The variable
c contains the conditioning information, such as the robot’s
observations and task instruction. Thus, xτ moves from noise
at τ = 0 to the data sample at τ = 1.
A flow-matching model learns a velocity field vθ(xτ, τ, c)
that predicts the direction from x0 to x1. At inference time,
the model begins with a noise sample and follows the learned
velocity field from τ = 0 to τ = 1. In an action policy, the
resulting sample x1 represents a predicted action or action
sequence.

The first term captures within-state uncertainty, i.e., the

overall diversity of possible actions, whereas divergence-based
(local) uncertainty isolates the ambiguity associated with the
particular trajectory being generated. This local characteri-
zation is particularly useful because trajectories under the
same context c can exhibit different levels of uncertainty.
Rather than assigning a single uncertainty value to the entire
conditional action distribution, Σdiv allows uncertainty to be
evaluated along individual trajectories, revealing where the
model encounters locally ambiguous decisions.
D. Analysis and Ablations
Sensitivity to Distribution-Shift Severity. We evaluate
all 10 LIBERO-Object tasks under LIBERO-Pro position
perturbations at multiple flow times. Figure 5 shows that
uncertainty increases monotonically from the clean condition
through perturbation level x0.4 at every evaluated flow time.
Sensitivity is greater at earlier, noisier flow states, with the
strongest separation at the earliest evaluated time, τ = 0.5.
At this stage, the latent contains less information about the
final action, so the vector field depends more strongly on
the observation and instruction; mismatches in the condi-
tioning are therefore more visible. Concurrently, success rate
decreases from 99.6% in the clean condition to 54.7%, 34.0%,
and 10.2% under progressively stronger perturbations. Thus,
divergence uncertainty tracks the severity of this controlled
position shift together with the associated degradation in policy
performance.
Fig. 5. Divergence uncertainty under increasing LIBERO-Pro position pertur-
bations. Curves show mean uncertainty at different flow times, error bars show
95% confidence intervals, and success rates are shown below the perturbation
levels.
Agreement with Action Entropy. We compare divergence
uncertainty with the sampling-based action entropy used by
AAC [15]. Figure 6 reports the Pearson correlation between
chunk-level scores over task progress and between per-action-
step uncertainty profiles. At τ = 0.9, the mean correlations
reach 0.88 and 0.80, respectively. At this later flow time, the
latent is close to the generated action trajectory, and the per-
step uncertainty profile is smoother and less dominated by
the initial latent noise. Divergence therefore broadly recovers
the uncertainty structure captured by action entropy once the
trajectory has largely formed.
